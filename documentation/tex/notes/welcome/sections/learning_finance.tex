Chapter \ref{chap.microstructure} builds the market from its mechanism.
An order-driven market matches orders by price and then by time,
and the limit order book is the state that rule leaves behind;
the best quotes, the spread, the mid-price and the queue imbalance are all read off it.
An exchange disseminates a stream of messages rather than a book,
so the book is recovered as a fold of that stream.
The stream is then modelled as a self-exciting point process,
whose intensity rises when an event of a related type has just occurred,
and Section \ref{sec.priceFormation} is what the chapter is for:
the order flow imbalance,
the statistic of that flow which both accounts for the mid-price and forecasts it.

Chapter \ref{chap.options} prices the contingent claim.
The Black--Scholes model and the replication argument behind it;
the fundamental theorem of derivative trading,
which says what a hedger earns
when the volatility hedged at differs from the one realised;
and the SABR model, which is what an option desk quotes a smile with.
Volatility is then traded in its own right, rather than as a view on direction.
