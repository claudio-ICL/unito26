The order flow imbalance is introduced in \cite{CKS14pri} through a book in which the
mechanism of Section \ref{sec.orderDrivenMarkets} is simplified until the mid-price becomes
an explicit function of the flow.
We state the simplification as an assumption, and the consequence as a proposition.

\begin{assumption}\label{assumption.idealisedBook}
 Over the window considered,
 \begin{enumerate}[label={\emph{\roman{*}}.}]
  \item every event occurs at the best quote of its side;
  \item each side holds the same depth $D > 0$ at every price level of the grid;
  \item the quote on each side moves by one tick for every $D$ shares of net flow on that
        side, in either direction --- downward on the bid when it is consumed or withdrawn,
        upward when a limit order improves it, and mutatis mutandis on the ask.
 \end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

Part \emph{iii.} is what makes the idealisation symmetric.
Without its second half a queue could only be depleted,
the bid could only fall and the ask only rise,
and the spread would widen without bound.

\begin{defi}[{\citealp{CKS14pri}}]\label{def.orderFlowImbalance}
 Let $\arrivalTimes[]_n$ be the times of the events of a trading epoch,
 and write $\nthBestBidSize[1]_{\arrivalTimes[]_n}$ for the size at the best bid
 \emph{after} the $n$-th event, so that
 $\nthBestBidSize[1]_{\arrivalTimes[]_{n-1}}
 = \nthBestBidSize[1]_{\arrivalTimes[]_n -}$.
 The contribution of the $n$-th event, for $n \geq 1$, is
 \begin{equation}
 \label{eq.orderFlowContribution}
 \begin{split}
  \orderFlowContribution
  =\;&
  \indicator{\bestBidPrice_{\arrivalTimes[]_n} \geq \bestBidPrice_{\arrivalTimes[]_{n-1}}}
  \nthBestBidSize[1]_{\arrivalTimes[]_n}
  -
  \indicator{\bestBidPrice_{\arrivalTimes[]_n} \leq \bestBidPrice_{\arrivalTimes[]_{n-1}}}
  \nthBestBidSize[1]_{\arrivalTimes[]_{n-1}}
  \\
  &-
  \indicator{\bestAskPrice_{\arrivalTimes[]_n} \leq \bestAskPrice_{\arrivalTimes[]_{n-1}}}
  \nthBestAskSize[1]_{\arrivalTimes[]_n}
  +
  \indicator{\bestAskPrice_{\arrivalTimes[]_n} \geq \bestAskPrice_{\arrivalTimes[]_{n-1}}}
  \nthBestAskSize[1]_{\arrivalTimes[]_{n-1}} ,
 \end{split}
 \end{equation}
 and the \emph{order flow imbalance} over the window $(t-w, t]$ is
 \begin{equation}
 \label{eq.orderFlowImbalance}
  \OFI_{t,w} = \sum_{n \, : \, t-w < \arrivalTimes[]_n \leq t} \orderFlowContribution .
 \end{equation}
\end{defi}

The index of every quantity in \eqref{eq.orderFlowContribution} is a physical time
$\arrivalTimes[]_n$ and not the counting index $n$,
which matters as soon as the arrival times are themselves modelled,
as they are in Section \ref{sec.pointProcesses}.

\begin{prop}\label{prop.idealisedUpdate}
 Under Assumption \ref{assumption.idealisedBook},
 the mid-price change over the window $(t-w,t]$ satisfies
 \begin{equation}\label{eq.idealisedUpdate}
  \Delta \midPrice_{t,w}
  = \frac{\tickSizeOfLOB}{2D} \, \OFI_{t,w} \; + \; R_{t,w},
  \qquad
  \vert R_{t,w} \vert \leq \tickSizeOfLOB ,
 \end{equation}
 where $R_{t,w}$ depends only on the sizes resting at the two touches at the endpoints of
 the window.
 In particular \eqref{eq.idealisedUpdate} is exact whenever those four sizes are equal to
 $D$.
\end{prop}
\begin{proof}
 By Assumption \ref{assumption.idealisedBook}~\emph{i.} every event changes the
 configuration only at the touch, so the update of Proposition \ref{prop.lobUpdate}
 is its restriction to the first level.
 Measure the position of the bid in shares by
 $\mathcal{Q}^{b}_{s} := \nthBestBidSize[1]_{s}
 + D\,(\bestBidPrice_{s} - \bestBidPrice_{t-w})/\tickSizeOfLOB$,
 one tick being worth $D$ shares by \emph{ii.} and \emph{iii.}
 Each event changes $\mathcal{Q}^{b}$ by exactly its own signed bid-side flow,
 whether it moves the quote or not,
 and the first two terms of \eqref{eq.orderFlowContribution} are that flow.
 Summing over the window,
 \begin{equation*}
  \frac{\bestBidPrice_{t} - \bestBidPrice_{t-w}}{\tickSizeOfLOB}
  = \frac{1}{D}\sum_{n} \Big(
  \text{bid terms of } \orderFlowContribution \Big)
  - \frac{\nthBestBidSize[1]_{t} - \nthBestBidSize[1]_{t-w}}{D} ,
 \end{equation*}
 and mutatis mutandis for the ask, with the opposite sign.
 Halving the sum of the two gives \eqref{eq.idealisedUpdate}, with
 $R_{t,w} = -\frac{\tickSizeOfLOB}{2D}\big[
 (\nthBestBidSize[1]_{t} - \nthBestBidSize[1]_{t-w})
 - (\nthBestAskSize[1]_{t} - \nthBestAskSize[1]_{t-w})\big]$.
 Each of the four sizes lies in $[0,D]$ by \emph{ii.}, whence the bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{remark.ofiUnits}
 Notice that $\OFI_{t,w}/(2D)$ is a number of shares over a number of shares,
 so \eqref{eq.idealisedUpdate} reads as a tick count on the left when $\tickSizeOfLOB$ is
 dropped, and as a price otherwise.
 Prices are carried as integer tick counts throughout these notes and throughout the code,
 and the conversion happens once, at the boundary.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}\label{remark.identityNotForecast}
 Notice that \eqref{eq.idealisedUpdate} is an identity about a window that has already
 elapsed, and not a forecast.
 Whether $\OFI_{t,w}$ says anything about the \emph{next} window is a different question,
 and Section \ref{sec.syntheticEvidence} shows that its answer is not a property of $\OFI$
 at all but of the kernel that generated the flow.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection*{The contrast and the imbalance}

The intensity contrast $\intensityContrast$ of Definition \ref{def.intensityContrast} and
the order flow imbalance are statistics of different things,
and neither is a coarse version of the other.

By \eqref{eq.intensityContrast}, $\intensityContrast$ is a function of the state
$\decayedCounts$ and of the parameters.
It is available only to an observer who knows $\excitation$ and $\decay$,
and under \eqref{eq.baselineSymmetry} it is blind to $\baseIntensity$,
so it says nothing about the immigrants that are about to arrive.

The imbalance is model free.
It counts events as they occurred, immigrants and offspring alike,
and it reads the book: the contribution \eqref{eq.orderFlowContribution} carries the size of
the order and the state of the queue it arrived at,
neither of which appears anywhere in $\hawkesKernel$.
The kernel governs when events happen and of which type;
the sizes govern how far they move the price.
No function of $\decayedCounts$ recovers a size.
