Equation \eqref{eq.idealisedUpdate} is exact for the flow of
Example \ref{example.idealisedPlacement}.
We drive that flow and the flow of Definition \ref{def.geometricPlacement}
with the same events from the same book,
and ask how far the identity carries over.
We proceed in three steps.
First we study the time at which the two mid-prices first differ.
Then we study the discrepancy of the mid-prices after that time:
its mean, and its variance.
Finally we ask what random order sizes change.

\subsubsection{Three flows on one path}
\label{sec.threeFlows}

\begin{defi}\label{def.threeBooks}
 Let $(\arrivalTimes[]_n, \event[n], \orderSize_n)_{n \geq 1}$ be the order flow of
 Definition \ref{def.orderFlowProcess},
 with no event before time zero,
 and let the \emph{opening book} have a spread of one tick
 and $L$ levels on each side,
 one on every tick,
 each holding $\uniformDepth$.
 Three books are folded from the opening book:
 \begin{enumerate}[label={\emph{\roman{*}}.}]
  \item the book $\cks$,
        by the messages that Example \ref{example.idealisedPlacement} makes of
        $(\arrivalTimes[]_n, \event[n])$;
  \item the book $\hawOne$,
        by the messages that Definition \ref{def.geometricPlacement} makes of
        $(\arrivalTimes[]_n, \event[n], 1)$;
  \item the book $\hawQ$,
        by the messages that Definition \ref{def.geometricPlacement} makes of
        $(\arrivalTimes[]_n, \event[n], \orderSize_n)$,
        from the opening book with every size multiplied by $\meanSize$.
 \end{enumerate}
\end{defi}

The opening book satisfies part \emph{iii.} of Assumption \ref{assumption.idealisedBook},
with both touches full,
so the book $\cks$ is the book of Example \ref{example.idealisedPlacement}:
our version of the book of \cite{CKS14pri},
along which \eqref{eq.idealisedUpdate} holds.
The books $\hawOne$ and $\hawQ$ are those of the Hawkes flow with the geometric rule for
prices,
with orders of one share and with the sizes of Definition \ref{def.orderFlowProcess},
which are lognormal, of mean $\meanSize$.
We give the three flows of messages the names of their books.
A quantity of a book carries its name as a superscript:
$\midPriceOf{\cks}_t$, $\nthBestBidSize[1,\hawOne]_t$, $\OFI^{\hawQ}_{t,t}$.
As in Definition \ref{def.orderFlowImbalance},
a quantity indexed by $\arrivalTimes[]_n$ is read after the $n$-th event,
and $\arrivalTimes[]_0 := 0$ indexes the opening book.
We write $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$ for the $\sigma$-algebra generated by the
arrival times and the types,
and $\size^{b}_t(\tickSizeOfLOB\N) := \sum_{p} \size^{b}_t(p)$
for the size resting on the whole bid,
and likewise $\size^{a}_t(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$ on the ask.

The placement of Example \ref{example.idealisedPlacement} draws nothing beyond the events,
so the book $\cks$ is $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$-measurable.
The geometric placement draws, for each event, an offset or the level of a withdrawal,
and the orders of $\hawQ$ carry a size besides.
Given $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$ and the draws of the earlier events,
these have the laws of Definitions \ref{def.orderFlowProcess}
and \ref{def.geometricPlacement},
the level of a withdrawal being drawn on the book as the earlier events left it.
We represent that draw by a variable $U_n$,
uniform on $(0,1)$ and independent of the path and of the other draws:
the withdrawal takes the first level of its side,
counted from the touch outwards,
at which the cumulated size exceeds $U_n$ times the size of the side.
This is the draw of Definition \ref{def.geometricPlacement}~\emph{iii.}
Write $\swapMatrix(e)$ for the type with which $\swapMatrix$ exchanges the type $e$.
The \emph{mirror} of the path is the path $(\arrivalTimes[]_n, \swapMatrix(\event[n]))_n$,
with the same $\priceOffset_n$, $U_n$ and $\orderSize_n$.

The books $\cks$ and $\hawOne$ receive the same events, with orders of one share,
and differ only in where the orders go.
The books $\hawOne$ and $\hawQ$ receive the same events and price them by the same rule,
and differ only in the size of the orders.
By Proposition \ref{prop.signedSizeContribution}
the imbalance of the book $\cks$ is the signed count $\pressure^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc$,
so that \eqref{eq.idealisedUpdate} counts events,
and a size other than one acts on it as an uncertainty in the observation of the size of
each order.
Setting $\cks$ against $\hawOne$ therefore isolates the placement of the orders,
and setting it against $\hawQ$ adds the uncertainty in their size.

\begin{assumption}\label{assumption.balancedFlow}
 The specification is direction-symmetric, with $\branchingRatio < 1$,
 the flow is \emph{balanced} on each side:
 \begin{equation*}
  \stationaryIntensity[3] = \stationaryIntensity[2] + \stationaryIntensity[5] ,
  \qquad
  \stationaryIntensity[4] = \stationaryIntensity[1] + \stationaryIntensity[6] ,
 \end{equation*}
 and the opening best bid lies above one tick.
\end{assumption}

By \eqref{eq.directionSymmetry} and \eqref{eq.stationaryIntensity},
$\swapMatrix\stationaryIntensity = \stationaryIntensity$,
so the two conditions of balance are one.

We study the three flows on books that stay well behaved.
Throughout, we work on the event $G$ that,
on the horizon considered,
on the path and on its mirror,
no side of any of the three books is empty
and no limit price meets the floor of Definition \ref{def.geometricPlacement}.
Every statement below is made on $G$,
and expectations and laws are taken on it;
we do not repeat it.
On a book of many levels, priced far above one tick,
the complement of $G$ has small probability,
and by its definition
$G$ is invariant under the exchange of the path with its mirror.

With orders of one share,
a withdrawal from an occupied side removes one share wherever it is drawn.
While the bid of $\hawOne$ is occupied,
a limit buy rests in full on it, by \eqref{eq.limitPrice},
a market sell removes one share from it,
and no other event changes it,
so that, with
$\sideVector{b} := \unitVector_3 - \unitVector_2 - \unitVector_5$
and
$\sideVector{a} := \unitVector_4 - \unitVector_1 - \unitVector_6$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq.sideSize}
 \size^{b,\hawOne}_t(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)
 = L\uniformDepth + (\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc(t) ,
 \qquad
 \size^{a,\hawOne}_t(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)
 = L\uniformDepth + (\sideVector{a})^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc(t) .
\end{equation}
The entries of $\sideVector{b}$ are the shares that an event of each type brings to the bid,
those of $\sideVector{a}$ the shares it brings to the ask,
and $\sideVector{b} - \sideVector{a} = \pressure$.
Under balance $(\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\stationaryIntensity = 0$,
so that by \eqref{eq.forwardMeanCountsDeviation} from no past
the mean of $(\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc(t)$ is bounded in $t$,
and by the argument of the proof of Proposition \ref{prop.forecastAtLongHorizons}
its variance grows as $t \, (\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\countCovarianceRate\,\sideVector{b}$.
A side of $L\uniformDepth$ shares therefore lasts a time of the order of
$(L\uniformDepth)^{2} / (\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\countCovarianceRate\,\sideVector{b}$,
which is $L^{2}$ times the time in which the same walk moves by $\uniformDepth$.

\begin{remark}\label{remark.wholeBookImbalance}
 By \eqref{eq.sideSize} and $\sideVector{b} - \sideVector{a} = \pressure$,
 \begin{equation*}
  \size^{b,\hawOne}_t(\tickSizeOfLOB\N) - \size^{a,\hawOne}_t(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)
  = \pressure^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc(t) :
 \end{equation*}
 the whole-book imbalance of $\hawOne$ is the signed count,
 whatever the offsets and the levels drawn.
 The book $\cks$ has the same whole-book imbalance.
 Every event changes the size on each side of $\cks$ as it changes that of $\hawOne$,
 except a limit order sent to the opposite touch,
 which takes one share from that side and adds none to its own:
 at each such order both sides of $\cks$ fall by one share relative to \eqref{eq.sideSize}.
 The book $\cks$ turns this imbalance into a mid-price at the fixed depth $\uniformDepth$ per
 tick;
 the book $\hawOne$ has no fixed depth.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{The first time the mid-prices differ}
\label{sec.partingTime}

The books $\cks$ and $\hawOne$ open from the same book and receive the same events,
so their mid-prices agree at first.
We ask when they first differ,
and which events make them differ.

\begin{defi}\label{def.partingTime}
 The \emph{parting time} of the books $\cks$ and $\hawOne$ is
 \begin{equation*}
  \partingTime := \inf \big\lbrace t \geq 0 :
  \midPriceOf{\cks}_t \neq \midPriceOf{\hawOne}_t \big\rbrace .
 \end{equation*}
\end{defi}

\begin{lemma}\label{lemma.oneBestPrice}
 In each of the three books an event moves at most one best price,
 up if $\pressure_{\event[n]} = +1$ and down if $\pressure_{\event[n]} = -1$.
 If the best prices of the books $\cks$ and $\hawOne$ agree before an event
 that moves a best price in both,
 it moves the same one in both.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
 We take the types $1$, $3$ and $5$;
 the types $2$, $4$ and $6$ are their mirrors,
 and the argument is the same mutatis mutandis.
 A market buy trades at the best ask in every book and changes nothing else:
 one share in the books $\cks$ and $\hawOne$,
 and $\orderSize_n$ shares, walking the ask, in $\hawQ$.
 Only the best ask can move,
 and removing the lowest asks can only raise it;
 $\pressure_1 = +1$.
 A limit buy of a geometric book is priced below the best ask by \eqref{eq.limitPrice},
 so it rests in full on the bid:
 only the best bid can move,
 and adding size raises it or leaves it.
 In the book $\cks$ a limit buy joins the best bid, or opens a level one tick above it,
 and the same holds;
 or, at a full best bid facing a spread of one tick,
 it trades one share at the best ask,
 and then only the best ask can move, up;
 $\pressure_3 = +1$.
 A withdrawal from the bid changes only the bid,
 and removing size can only lower the best bid or leave it;
 $\pressure_5 = -1$.

 A type moves different best prices in the books $\cks$ and $\hawOne$
 only when $\cks$ sends a limit order to the opposite touch,
 at a spread of one tick.
 If the best prices agree before the event,
 the spread of $\hawOne$ is then one tick as well,
 and \eqref{eq.limitPrice} prices a limit buy of $\hawOne$ at
 $\bestBidPrice_{\arrivalTimes[]_n-} - \tickSizeOfLOB\priceOffset_n$,
 at or behind the best bid,
 so that it moves no best price.
\end{proof}

\begin{corol}\label{corol.partingAtOneQuote}
 The parting time $\partingTime$ is the first time
 at which a best price of the book $\cks$ differs from that of $\hawOne$,
 and at $\partingTime$ exactly one of the two best prices differs.
\end{corol}
\begin{proof}
 Exercise \ref{exercise.partingAtOneQuote}.
\end{proof}

\begin{exercise}\label{exercise.partingAtOneQuote}
 Prove Corollary \ref{corol.partingAtOneQuote}.
 Show from Lemma \ref{lemma.oneBestPrice} that,
 if the best prices of the two books agree before an event,
 at most one of them differs after it,
 and that the mid-prices then differ exactly when one does.
\end{exercise}

The queues of the two books may differ long before $\partingTime$,
and the mid-prices do not see them:
the parting time is decided by the moves of the best prices alone.

\begin{prop}\label{prop.partingProbability}
 Let the best prices of the books $\cks$ and $\hawOne$ agree before the $n$-th event,
 at a spread of $k$ ticks.
 Call the \emph{side of the event} the side on which its message in $\cks$ acts:
 the opposite side for a market order,
 and for a limit order at a full touch facing a spread of one tick;
 its own side otherwise.
 Reading the books before the event,
 let $\size^{\cks}$ and $\size^{\hawOne}$ be the sizes at the touches of that side,
 $\size^{\hawOne}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$ the size resting on it in $\hawOne$,
 and $g$ the number of ticks from the touch of $\hawOne$ to the next occupied price behind it.
 Each book moves the best price of the side of the event,
 by $j^{\cks}$ and $j^{\hawOne}$ ticks,
 and no other best price.
 The event parts the mid-prices exactly when $j^{\cks} \neq j^{\hawOne}$.
 For a limit order the \emph{own touch} is the touch of $\cks$ on the side of the order.
 Given $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$ and the draws of the earlier events,
 $j^{\cks}$ and the probability that the event parts the mid-prices are
 \begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{lll}
   \toprule
   event & $j^{\cks}$ & probability of parting \\
   \midrule
   market order, $\size^{\cks} \geq 2$
   & $0$
   & $\indicator{\size^{\hawOne} = 1}$ \\
   market order, $\size^{\cks} = 1$
   & $1$
   & $1 - \indicator{\size^{\hawOne} = 1, \, g = 1}$ \\
   limit order, $k = 1$, own touch $< \uniformDepth$
   & $0$
   & $0$ \\
   limit order, $k = 1$, own touch $= \uniformDepth$
   & $\indicator{\size^{\cks} = 1}$
   & $\indicator{\size^{\cks} = 1}$ \\
   limit order, $k \geq 2$, $\size^{\cks} < \uniformDepth$
   & $0$
   & $1 - (1-\offsetParameter)^{k-1}$ \\
   limit order, $k \geq 2$, $\size^{\cks} = \uniformDepth$
   & $1$
   & $1 - \offsetParameter(1-\offsetParameter)^{k-2}$ \\
   withdrawal, $\size^{\cks} \geq 2$
   & $0$
   & $\indicator{\size^{\hawOne} = 1} / \size^{\hawOne}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$ \\
   withdrawal, $\size^{\cks} = 1$
   & $1$
   & $1 - \indicator{\size^{\hawOne} = 1, \, g = 1} / \size^{\hawOne}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$ \\
   \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
 \end{center}
\end{prop}
\begin{proof}
 By Lemma \ref{lemma.oneBestPrice} and Corollary \ref{corol.partingAtOneQuote},
 the event parts the mid-prices exactly when the two books move their best prices
 differently.
 By the proof of Lemma \ref{lemma.oneBestPrice},
 each book moves at most the best price of the side of the event:
 the book $\hawOne$ moves no best price when $\cks$ sends a limit order to the opposite
 touch.
 The event therefore parts the mid-prices exactly when $j^{\cks} \neq j^{\hawOne}$.

 \emph{The moves of $\cks$.}
 The opening book has a level on every tick,
 and the placement of Example \ref{example.idealisedPlacement} opens a level only one tick
 inside a touch and empties one only at a touch,
 so each side of $\cks$ holds a level on every tick behind its touch,
 of $\uniformDepth$ shares by part \emph{iii.} of Assumption \ref{assumption.idealisedBook}.
 A best price of $\cks$ therefore moves by one tick or not at all.
 A market order or a withdrawal moves the touch it takes from
 exactly when that touch holds one share.
 A limit order at $k \geq 2$ opens a level one tick inside its touch
 exactly when that touch holds $\uniformDepth$,
 and joins it otherwise.
 At $k = 1$ it joins its touch if that holds less than $\uniformDepth$,
 and otherwise trades one share at the opposite touch,
 the touch of the side of the event,
 which then moves exactly when it holds one share.

 \emph{The moves of $\hawOne$.}
 A market order takes one share from the touch,
 which moves by $g$ ticks exactly when it held one share.
 A limit order at $k \geq 2$ is priced $k - 1 - \priceOffset_n$ ticks inside the touch of
 its side by \eqref{eq.limitPrice},
 so it moves that touch by $j \in \lbrace 1, \dots, k-1 \rbrace$ ticks with probability
 $\offsetParameter(1-\offsetParameter)^{k-1-j}$, by \eqref{eq.priceOffset},
 and leaves it with probability $(1-\offsetParameter)^{k-1}$;
 at $k = 1$ it moves no best price.
 A withdrawal moves the touch exactly when it draws the touch,
 which it does with probability $\size^{\hawOne}/\size^{\hawOne}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$,
 and the touch holds one share;
 it then moves it by $g$ ticks.

 The offsets are independent of $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$ and of the earlier
 draws,
 and the level of a withdrawal is drawn in proportion to the sizes of the book before the
 event,
 which is a function of $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$ and of the earlier draws.
 Each entry of the table is the probability that the two moves differ:
 for a market order,
 $j^{\hawOne} \neq 0$ exactly when $\size^{\hawOne} = 1$,
 and $j^{\hawOne} = 1$ exactly when moreover $g = 1$;
 for a limit order at $k = 1$, $j^{\hawOne} = 0$;
 at $k \geq 2$,
 $j^{\hawOne} \neq 0$ with probability $1 - (1-\offsetParameter)^{k-1}$
 and $j^{\hawOne} \neq 1$ with probability $1 - \offsetParameter(1-\offsetParameter)^{k-2}$;
 for a withdrawal,
 $j^{\hawOne} \neq 0$ with probability
 $\indicator{\size^{\hawOne} = 1} / \size^{\hawOne}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$
 and $j^{\hawOne} = 1$ with probability
 $\indicator{\size^{\hawOne} = 1, \, g = 1} / \size^{\hawOne}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$.
 In both books a market order acts on the side of the event
 as a withdrawal that draws the touch,
 and its rows are those of the withdrawal
 with the probability $\size^{\hawOne}/\size^{\hawOne}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$ of that draw
 set to one.
\end{proof}

At a spread of one tick the offsets play no part in the table:
a limit order parts the mid-prices only when $\cks$ sends it to an opposite touch of one
share,
and a market order or a withdrawal only when it takes the last share of a touch in one book
at least.

\begin{corol}\label{corol.quietBook}
 Let $\firstMove^{\cks}$ and $\firstMove^{\hawOne}$ be the first times at which a best price
 of the book $\cks$ and of the book $\hawOne$ moves.
 Then both books keep the opening best prices on
 $[0, \firstMove^{\cks} \wedge \firstMove^{\hawOne})$,
 the first move of each is the first event that takes the last share of one of its touches,
 and
 \begin{equation*}
  \partingTime = \firstMove^{\cks} \wedge \firstMove^{\hawOne}
 \end{equation*}
 unless the two books make the same move at that event.
 In particular, while the spread is one tick
 and every touch of the two books holds at least two shares,
 no event moves a best price of either:
 we say that the books are \emph{quiet}.
\end{corol}
\begin{proof}
 Before $\firstMove^{\cks} \wedge \firstMove^{\hawOne}$ no best price has moved,
 so both books keep the opening best prices and the spread of one tick.
 At a spread of one tick, by the proof of Proposition \ref{prop.partingProbability},
 a best price of $\cks$ moves exactly when a market order or a withdrawal takes the last
 share of a touch,
 or a limit order sent to the opposite touch takes the last share there;
 and a best price of $\hawOne$ moves exactly when a market order or a withdrawal takes the
 last share of a touch.
 This gives the second claim and the last.
 The mid-prices agree before $\firstMove^{\cks} \wedge \firstMove^{\hawOne}$,
 and at that event at least one book moves a best price;
 by Corollary \ref{corol.partingAtOneQuote} the mid-prices then differ,
 unless the other book makes the same move.
\end{proof}

The same move needs a touch of one share in both books at the same event,
emptied by the same market order or withdrawal,
with $g = 1$ in $\hawOne$.

Before its first move the book $\cks$ is a function of two walks.
Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq.touchWalks}
 \touchWalk{b}(t) := \uniformDepth + (\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc(t) ,
 \qquad
 \touchWalk{a}(t) := \uniformDepth + (\sideVector{a})^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc(t)
\end{equation}
be the \emph{touch walks},
and let
\begin{equation*}
 \walkMaximum(t) := \max \Big( \uniformDepth ,
 \max_{s \leq t} \max\big( \touchWalk{b}(s), \touchWalk{a}(s) \big) \Big)
\end{equation*}
be their running maximum.

\begin{prop}\label{prop.cksTouchWalks}
 For $t < \firstMove^{\cks}$,
 \begin{equation*}
  \nthBestBidSize[1,\cks]_t
  = \touchWalk{b}(t) - \big( \walkMaximum(t) - \uniformDepth \big) ,
  \qquad
  \nthBestAskSize[1,\cks]_t
  = \touchWalk{a}(t) - \big( \walkMaximum(t) - \uniformDepth \big) ,
 \end{equation*}
 and
 \begin{equation*}
  \firstMove^{\cks}
  = \inf \Big\lbrace t :
  \walkMaximum(t) - \min\big( \touchWalk{b}(t), \touchWalk{a}(t) \big) = \uniformDepth
  \Big\rbrace .
 \end{equation*}
 In particular $\firstMove^{\cks}$ is $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$-measurable,
 and under balance the means of $\touchWalk{b}(t) - \uniformDepth$
 and $\touchWalk{a}(t) - \uniformDepth$ are bounded in $t$.
\end{prop}
\begin{proof}
 Write $x^{b}$ and $x^{a}$ for the two right-hand sides,
 both equal to $\uniformDepth$ at $t = 0$,
 and both at most $\uniformDepth$, since $\walkMaximum$ dominates both walks.
 We argue by induction on the events before $\firstMove^{\cks}$,
 before which no best price of the book $\cks$ has moved,
 so that its spread is one tick.
 A limit buy raises $\touchWalk{b}$ by one,
 and raises $\walkMaximum$ by one exactly when $\touchWalk{b} = \walkMaximum$ before it,
 that is when $x^{b} = \uniformDepth$.
 If $x^{b} < \uniformDepth$ the order joins the best bid,
 and $x^{b}$ rises by one, as the best bid does.
 If $x^{b} = \uniformDepth$ the best bid is full,
 the order trades one share at the best ask,
 and $x^{b}$ stays while $x^{a}$ falls by one, as the two touches do.
 A market sell and a withdrawal from the bid lower $\touchWalk{b}$ by one,
 leave $\walkMaximum$ unchanged,
 and take one share from the best bid.
 The types of the ask are the same mutatis mutandis.
 At a spread of one tick,
 by the proof of Proposition \ref{prop.partingProbability},
 a best price of $\cks$ moves exactly at an event that takes the last share of a touch,
 at which $\min(x^{b}, x^{a})$ reaches zero;
 each event changes $x^{b}$ and $x^{a}$ by at most one,
 which gives the formula for $\firstMove^{\cks}$.
 From no past, \eqref{eq.forwardMeanCountsDeviation} gives
 $\Expectation\,\multiCountingProc(t) - t\,\stationaryIntensity
 = -\excitation\,\meanResponseIntegral(t)\,m$,
 with $m$ the stationary mean of the state,
 which is bounded in $t$ since $\branchingRatio < 1$,
 and $(\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\stationaryIntensity
 = (\sideVector{a})^{\transpose}\stationaryIntensity = 0$ under balance.
\end{proof}

The touch walks count what arrives at each touch.
The cap of part \emph{iii.} of Assumption \ref{assumption.idealisedBook}
removes from both the same quantity $\walkMaximum - \uniformDepth$,
which is the number of limit orders that $\cks$ has sent to the opposite touch,
and so couples the two sides:
$\firstMove^{\cks}$ is the first time one walk falls $\uniformDepth$ below the running
maximum of both.

Before $\firstMove^{\hawOne}$ the spread of $\hawOne$ is one tick as well,
and its best bid evolves by
\begin{equation}\label{eq.hawkesTouch}
 \nthBestBidSize[1,\hawOne]_t
 = \uniformDepth
 + \sum_{n \, : \, \arrivalTimes[]_n \leq t, \, \event[n] = 3}
 \indicator{\priceOffset_n = 0}
 - \countingProc[2](t)
 - \sum_{n \, : \, \arrivalTimes[]_n \leq t, \, \event[n] = 5}
 \indicator{p_n = \bestBidPrice_{\arrivalTimes[]_n-}} ,
\end{equation}
with no cap:
a limit buy joins the touch when $\priceOffset_n = 0$, with probability $\offsetParameter$,
and goes behind it otherwise;
a market sell takes one share from it;
and a withdrawal from the bid draws it with probability
$\nthBestBidSize[1,\hawOne]_{\arrivalTimes[]_n-}
/ \size^{b,\hawOne}_{\arrivalTimes[]_n-}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$,
the denominator being \eqref{eq.sideSize}.
As $\uniformDepth$ grows,
over horizons proportional to $\uniformDepth$,
the counts follow their means,
and the touch follows an ordinary differential equation.

\begin{prop}\label{prop.fluidTouch}
 Let $\uniformDepth \to \infty$, with $L$, $\offsetParameter$ and the specification fixed.
 Let $\fluidTouch$ solve
 \begin{equation}\label{eq.fluidTouch}
  \fluidTouch\derivative(s)
  = \offsetParameter\stationaryIntensity[3] - \stationaryIntensity[2]
  - \frac{\stationaryIntensity[5]}{L} \, \fluidTouch(s) ,
  \qquad
  \fluidTouch(0) = 1 ,
 \end{equation}
 that is
 $\fluidTouch(s) = \fluidTouch_{\infty}
 + (1 - \fluidTouch_{\infty}) \, e^{-\stationaryIntensity[5] s / L}$
 with
 $\fluidTouch_{\infty} := L \, (\offsetParameter\stationaryIntensity[3]
 - \stationaryIntensity[2]) / \stationaryIntensity[5]$,
 and let $s_1 > 0$ be such that $\fluidTouch > 0$ on $[0, s_1]$.
 Then, in probability,
 \begin{equation*}
  \sup_{s \leq s_1} \Big\vert
  \frac{\nthBestBidSize[1,\hawOne]_{\uniformDepth s}}{\uniformDepth} - \fluidTouch(s)
  \Big\vert \to 0 ,
  \qquad
  \Prob\big( \firstMove^{\hawOne} > \uniformDepth s_1 \big) \to 1 ,
 \end{equation*}
 and the same holds of the best ask.
\end{prop}
\begin{proof}
 We proceed in three steps.

 \emph{A law of large numbers for the counts.}
 Let $\martingale := \multiCountingProc - \compensator$.
 From no past $\decayedCounts(0+) = 0$,
 and integrating \eqref{eq.stateDynamics} and \eqref{eq.intensityFromState} over $(0, t]$,
 as in the derivation of \eqref{eq.countIncrementIdentity},
 gives at every $t$
 \begin{equation}\label{eq.countsFromNoPast}
  \multiCountingProc(t) - t\,\stationaryIntensity
  = (I - \branchingMatrix)^{-1} \martingale(t)
  - \branchingMatrix (I - \branchingMatrix)^{-1} \decayedCounts(t+) .
 \end{equation}
 By Doob's inequality\footnote{%
  Doob's inequality bounds a square-integrable martingale $X$ by
  $\Expectation \sup_{t \leq T} X(t)^{2} \leq 4\,\Expectation\,X(T)^{2}$.
  Gronwall's inequality, in the form used below,
  states that a bounded function $f \geq 0$ with
  $f(s) \leq a + b \int_{(0,s]} f(s'-) \, \mu(ds')$,
  for constants $a, b \geq 0$ and a measure $\mu$ with finitely many atoms on $(0,s]$,
  satisfies $f(s) \leq a \, e^{b \mu((0,s])}$.}
 $\Expectation \sup_{t \leq T} \martingale_e(t)^{2}
 \leq 4\,\Expectation\,\martingale_e(T)^{2} = 4\,\Expectation\,\countingProc[e](T) = O(T)$,
 by \eqref{eq.forwardMeanCountsDeviation}.
 The proof of Theorem \ref{thm.lyapunov} starts from $\decayedCounts(0) = 0$
 and shows that the second moment of the state converges,
 so the second moments of $\decayedCounts(t+)$ are bounded in $t$,
 and so are those of
 $\countingProc[e](k+1) - \countingProc[e](k)
 = \decayedCounts_e((k+1)+) - \decayedCounts_e(k+)
 + \decay \int_{k}^{k+1} \decayedCounts_e(t) \, dt$.
 Since
 $\sup_{t \in (k, k+1]} \decayedCounts_e(t+)
 \leq \decayedCounts_e(k+) + \countingProc[e](k+1) - \countingProc[e](k)$,
 Chebyshev's inequality and a union bound over the unit intervals of $[0, T]$ give
 $\Prob\big( \sup_{t \leq T} \vert \decayedCounts(t+) \vert > T^{3/4} \big)
 = O(T^{-1/2})$.
 Taking $T = \uniformDepth s_1$ in \eqref{eq.countsFromNoPast},
 and bounding its martingale term by Doob's and Chebyshev's inequalities,
 we find, with
 $\multiCountingProc^{\uniformDepth}(s) := \multiCountingProc(\uniformDepth s)/\uniformDepth$,
 \begin{equation*}
  \sup_{s \leq s_1} \big\vert
  \multiCountingProc^{\uniformDepth}(s) - s\,\stationaryIntensity
  \big\vert \to 0
 \end{equation*}
 in probability,
 and in particular, by \eqref{eq.sideSize} and balance,
 $\size^{b,\hawOne}_{\uniformDepth s}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N) / \uniformDepth \to L$
 uniformly on $[0, s_1]$.

 \emph{The draws.}
 For a limit buy let $\delta_n := \indicator{\priceOffset_n = 0} - \offsetParameter$,
 for a withdrawal from the bid
 $\delta_n := \indicator{p_n = \bestBidPrice_{\arrivalTimes[]_n-}}
 - \nthBestBidSize[1,\hawOne]_{\arrivalTimes[]_n-}
 / \size^{b,\hawOne}_{\arrivalTimes[]_n-}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$,
 and $\delta_n := 0$ for the other types.
 Given $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$ and the draws of the events before the $n$-th,
 $\delta_n$ has mean zero, by Definition \ref{def.geometricPlacement},
 and $\vert \delta_n \vert \leq 1$.
 Given $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$,
 the partial sums $J_3(t)$ of the $\delta_n$ over the limit buys of $(0, t]$,
 and $J_5(t)$ over the withdrawals from the bid,
 therefore form martingales in the order of the events,
 with orthogonal increments,
 and Doob's inequality gives
 $\Expectation \sup_{t \leq T} J_e(t)^{2} \leq 4\,\Expectation\,\countingProc[e](T) = O(T)$,
 so that $\sup_{s \leq s_1} \vert J_e(\uniformDepth s) \vert / \uniformDepth \to 0$ in
 probability, for $e = 3, 5$.

 \emph{Gronwall's inequality.}
 Write
 $x(s) := \nthBestBidSize[1,\hawOne]_{\uniformDepth s} / \uniformDepth$
 and
 $y(s) := \size^{b,\hawOne}_{\uniformDepth s}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N) / \uniformDepth$,
 and let $\tilde{x}(s)$ be the right-hand side of \eqref{eq.hawkesTouch} at
 $t = \uniformDepth s$, divided by $\uniformDepth$,
 for $s \leq \firstMove^{\hawOne}/\uniformDepth$.
 Then $\tilde{x} = x$ on $[0, \firstMove^{\hawOne}/\uniformDepth)$,
 and $\tilde{x}(\firstMove^{\hawOne}/\uniformDepth) = 0$,
 the event at $\firstMove^{\hawOne}$ taking the last share of the touch.
 By the definition of $J_3$ and $J_5$,
 \begin{equation*}
  \tilde{x}(s)
  = 1 + \offsetParameter\, \countingProc[3]^{\uniformDepth}(s)
  - \countingProc[2]^{\uniformDepth}(s)
  - \int_{(0,s]} \frac{\tilde{x}(s'-)}{y(s'-)} \, d\countingProc[5]^{\uniformDepth}(s')
  + \frac{J_{3}(\uniformDepth s) - J_{5}(\uniformDepth s)}{\uniformDepth} ,
 \end{equation*}
 while
 $\fluidTouch(s) = 1 + (\offsetParameter\stationaryIntensity[3] - \stationaryIntensity[2]) s
 - \int_{0}^{s} \stationaryIntensity[5] \fluidTouch(s')/L \, ds'$.
 Subtract,
 and use
 $\vert \tilde{x}/y - \fluidTouch/L \vert
 \leq \vert \tilde{x} - \fluidTouch \vert / y + \vert \fluidTouch \vert \,
 \vert 1/y - 1/L \vert$
 with $y \geq L/2$,
 which holds on $[0, s_1]$ with probability tending to one by step 1.
 For $s \leq s_1 \wedge \firstMove^{\hawOne}/\uniformDepth$ this gives
 \begin{equation*}
  \vert \tilde{x}(s) - \fluidTouch(s) \vert
  \leq \bar{\delta}_{\uniformDepth}
  + \frac{2}{L} \int_{(0,s]} \vert \tilde{x}(s'-) - \fluidTouch(s') \vert \,
  d\countingProc[5]^{\uniformDepth}(s') ,
 \end{equation*}
 where $\bar{\delta}_{\uniformDepth}$ is the supremum over $s \leq s_1$ of
 $\offsetParameter \vert \countingProc[3]^{\uniformDepth}(s) - \stationaryIntensity[3] s \vert
 + \vert \countingProc[2]^{\uniformDepth}(s) - \stationaryIntensity[2] s \vert
 + \vert J_3(\uniformDepth s) - J_5(\uniformDepth s) \vert / \uniformDepth
 + \sup_{[0,s_1]} \vert \fluidTouch \vert \, \sup_{[0,s_1]} \vert 1/y - 1/L \vert \,
 \countingProc[5]^{\uniformDepth}(s_1)
 + \vert \int_{0}^{s} \fluidTouch(s') \,
 ( d\countingProc[5]^{\uniformDepth}(s') - \stationaryIntensity[5] \, ds' ) \vert / L$.
 The first four terms tend to zero in probability by steps 1 and 2;
 so does the last,
 which an integration by parts writes as
 $\vert \fluidTouch(s) ( \countingProc[5]^{\uniformDepth}(s) - \stationaryIntensity[5] s )
 - \int_{0}^{s} \fluidTouch\derivative(s')
 ( \countingProc[5]^{\uniformDepth}(s') - \stationaryIntensity[5] s' ) \, ds' \vert / L$.
 Gronwall's inequality for the measure $d\countingProc[5]^{\uniformDepth}$,
 whose atoms are $1/\uniformDepth$,
 gives
 \begin{equation*}
  \sup_{s \leq s_1 \wedge \firstMove^{\hawOne}/\uniformDepth}
  \vert \tilde{x}(s) - \fluidTouch(s) \vert
  \leq \bar{\delta}_{\uniformDepth} \, e^{2 \countingProc[5]^{\uniformDepth}(s_1)/L} \to 0
 \end{equation*}
 in probability.
 On $\lbrace \firstMove^{\hawOne} \leq \uniformDepth s_1 \rbrace$
 the left-hand side is at least
 $\fluidTouch(\firstMove^{\hawOne}/\uniformDepth) \geq \min_{[0,s_1]} \fluidTouch > 0$,
 since $\tilde{x}$ vanishes at $\firstMove^{\hawOne}/\uniformDepth$.
 Hence $\Prob(\firstMove^{\hawOne} > \uniformDepth s_1) \to 1$,
 and on that event $x = \tilde{x}$ on $[0, s_1]$,
 which is the first claim.
 The ask is the same, with the types $4$, $1$ and $6$,
 whose stationary intensities are those of $3$, $2$ and $5$.
\end{proof}

\begin{exercise}\label{exercise.fluidTouch}
 Solve \eqref{eq.fluidTouch}.
 Show that $\fluidTouch > 0$ on $[0, \infty)$ if and only if
 $\offsetParameter\stationaryIntensity[3] \geq \stationaryIntensity[2]$,
 and that otherwise $\fluidTouch$ reaches zero at the time $\fluidEmptying$ of
 \eqref{eq.fluidEmptying}.
\end{exercise}

\begin{corol}\label{corol.twoRegimes}
 Let $\uniformDepth \to \infty$ as in Proposition \ref{prop.fluidTouch}.
 \begin{enumerate}[label={\emph{\roman{*}}.}]
  \item If $\offsetParameter\stationaryIntensity[3] < \stationaryIntensity[2]$,
        the fluid touch reaches zero at
        \begin{equation}\label{eq.fluidEmptying}
         \fluidEmptying
         = \frac{L}{\stationaryIntensity[5]}
         \log \Big( 1 + \frac{\stationaryIntensity[5]}
         {L \, (\stationaryIntensity[2] - \offsetParameter\stationaryIntensity[3])} \Big) ,
        \end{equation}
        and $\partingTime/\uniformDepth \to \fluidEmptying$
        and $\firstMove^{\hawOne}/\uniformDepth \to \fluidEmptying$ in probability,
        with $\Prob(\partingTime = \firstMove^{\hawOne}) \to 1$:
        the touch of $\hawOne$ empties first,
        and the first move is that of $\hawOne$.
  \item If $\offsetParameter\stationaryIntensity[3] \geq \stationaryIntensity[2]$,
        the fluid touch is positive on $[0, \infty)$,
        and $\partingTime/\uniformDepth \to \infty$ in probability.
 \end{enumerate}
\end{corol}
\begin{proof}
 By step 1 of the proof of Proposition \ref{prop.fluidTouch} and balance,
 $\sup_{t \leq \uniformDepth s_1}
 \vert \touchWalk{b}(t) - \uniformDepth \vert / \uniformDepth$
 and its ask counterpart tend to zero in probability for every $s_1$,
 so that, with probability tending to one,
 $\walkMaximum - \min(\touchWalk{b}, \touchWalk{a}) < \uniformDepth$
 on $[0, \uniformDepth s_1]$.
 By Proposition \ref{prop.cksTouchWalks}, then,
 $\Prob(\firstMove^{\cks} > \uniformDepth s_1) \to 1$,
 and the touches of $\cks$ hold at least two shares on $[0, \uniformDepth s_1]$
 with probability tending to one.

 \emph{i.}
 The formula for $\fluidEmptying$ is Exercise \ref{exercise.fluidTouch}.
 Let $s_{-} < \fluidEmptying < s_{+}$.
 Proposition \ref{prop.fluidTouch} on $[0, s_{-}]$ gives
 $\Prob\big( \firstMove^{\hawOne} > \uniformDepth s_{-} \big) \to 1$.
 Its proof, up to its last display,
 does not use the sign of $\fluidTouch$;
 run on $[0, s_{+}]$, where $\fluidTouch$ continues the solution,
 it shows that on
 $\lbrace \firstMove^{\hawOne} > \uniformDepth s_{+} \rbrace$
 the touch at $\uniformDepth s_{+}$ is close to
 $\uniformDepth\fluidTouch(s_{+}) < 0$,
 which a size cannot be,
 so that
 $\Prob\big( \firstMove^{\hawOne} \leq \uniformDepth s_{+} \big) \to 1$.
 With probability tending to one, therefore,
 $\firstMove^{\hawOne} < \firstMove^{\cks}$,
 and at $\firstMove^{\hawOne}$ the touches of $\cks$ hold at least two shares,
 so that $\cks$ does not make the same move,
 and Corollary \ref{corol.quietBook} gives $\partingTime = \firstMove^{\hawOne}$.

 \emph{ii.}
 Here $\fluidTouch > 0$ on every $[0, s_1]$,
 and Proposition \ref{prop.fluidTouch} gives
 $\Prob(\firstMove^{\hawOne} > \uniformDepth s_1) \to 1$ for every $s_1$.
 By Corollary \ref{corol.quietBook},
 $\partingTime \geq \firstMove^{\cks} \wedge \firstMove^{\hawOne}$,
 and both terms exceed $\uniformDepth s_1$ with probability tending to one.
\end{proof}

The regime is decided by the sign of
$\offsetParameter\stationaryIntensity[3] - \stationaryIntensity[2]$:
the rate at which limit buys join the best bid of $\hawOne$
against the rate at which market sells take from it.
The withdrawals take from the touch in proportion to its share of the side,
and pull it towards $\fluidTouch_{\infty}\uniformDepth$.
In the first regime the book $\cks$ plays no part in $\partingTime$.
In the second the proof of Corollary \ref{corol.twoRegimes} stops at the fluid scale,
and what follows it is an approximation.

\begin{approximation}\label{approx.secondRegime}
 Let $\offsetParameter\stationaryIntensity[3] > \stationaryIntensity[2]$,
 and let $\uniformDepth$ be large.
 By \eqref{eq.hawkesTouch},
 the touch of $\hawOne$ relaxes,
 at the rate $\stationaryIntensity[5]/(L\uniformDepth)$ per share of deviation,
 towards $(\fluidTouch_{\infty}/L) \, \size^{b,\hawOne}_t(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$,
 which by \eqref{eq.sideSize} is $\fluidTouch_{\infty}\uniformDepth$
 plus a multiple of a walk without drift,
 and it fluctuates about that centre by $O(\sqrt{\uniformDepth})$.
 It reaches zero by a large deviation of the fluctuation,
 or when the side is nearly exhausted,
 which by \eqref{eq.sideSize} happens at the scale
 $(L\uniformDepth)^{2} / (\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\countCovarianceRate\,\sideVector{b}$.
 The book $\cks$ moves at the diffusive scale:
 \begin{equation*}
  \frac{1}{\uniformDepth} \Big(
  \touchWalk{b}(\uniformDepth^{2} s) - \uniformDepth ,
  \touchWalk{a}(\uniformDepth^{2} s) - \uniformDepth \Big)
 \end{equation*}
 converges in law to a Brownian motion $(\brownianMotion^{b}, \brownianMotion^{a})$ with
 $\CoVariance\big( \brownianMotion^{x}(s), \brownianMotion^{y}(s) \big)
 = s \, (\sideVector{x})^{\transpose}\countCovarianceRate\,\sideVector{y}$
 for $x, y \in \lbrace b, a \rbrace$,
 and $\firstMove^{\cks}/\uniformDepth^{2}$ converges in law to the first $s$ at which
 \begin{equation*}
  \max \Big( 0 , \sup_{s' \leq s}
  \max\big( \brownianMotion^{b}(s'), \brownianMotion^{a}(s') \big) \Big)
  - \min\big( \brownianMotion^{b}(s), \brownianMotion^{a}(s) \big) = 1 .
 \end{equation*}
 Both scales are of the order of $\uniformDepth^{2}$,
 and they stand in the ratio of $\Expectation\,\firstMove^{\cks}$
 to $L^{2}\uniformDepth^{2} / (\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\countCovarianceRate\,\sideVector{b}$.
 Away from the threshold, and for $L$ large,
 $\partingTime = \firstMove^{\cks}$ with probability close to one,
 and $\partingTime$ is then a function of the path,
 of the order of
 $\uniformDepth^{2} / (\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\countCovarianceRate\,\sideVector{b}$.
\end{approximation}

The convergence of the touch walks is the functional central limit theorem
of \cite{BDHM13som},
applied to $(\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc$
and $(\sideVector{a})^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc$,
whose drifts vanish under balance.
The approximation rests on two statements that are not proved here.
The first is the behaviour of the touch of $\hawOne$,
as an Ornstein--Uhlenbeck process about a moving centre,
with a noise of order one per unit of time
and a stationary variance of the order of $\uniformDepth$,
for which a fall of the order of $\uniformDepth$ is a large deviation.
The second is the continuity of the functional that maps the two walks to their first
drawdown of one unit,
at the paths of a Brownian motion,
which turns the convergence in law of the walks into that of $\firstMove^{\cks}$.

\subsubsection{The discrepancy of the mid-prices}
\label{sec.discrepancy}

After the parting time the two mid-prices move apart.
We study their difference:
its mean, which is zero,
and its variance,
which we split into the part that the path of events decides
and the part that the placement of the orders adds.

\begin{defi}\label{def.discrepancy}
 The \emph{discrepancy} of the books $\cks$ and $\hawOne$ is
 \begin{equation*}
  \discrepancy_t := \midPriceOf{\cks}_t - \midPriceOf{\hawOne}_t .
 \end{equation*}
\end{defi}

Both mid-prices lie on the grid $\tfrac{\tickSizeOfLOB}{2}\Z$ of half ticks,
and so does $\discrepancy_t$.

\begin{prop}\label{prop.discrepancyMoments}
 For every $t$ on the horizon considered:
 \begin{enumerate}[label={\emph{\roman{*}}.}]
  \item $\discrepancy_t = 0$ on $\lbrace \partingTime > t \rbrace$;
  \item the law of $\discrepancy_t$ is symmetric about zero,
        and in particular $\Expectation\,\discrepancy_t = 0$;
  \item $\Variance\,\discrepancy_t
        = \Expectation\big[ \discrepancy_t^{2} ; \, \partingTime \leq t \big]
        \geq (\tickSizeOfLOB/2)^{2} \, \Prob(\discrepancy_t \neq 0)$;
  \item $\Variance\,\discrepancy_t
        = \Variance\big( \Expectation[ \discrepancy_t \mid \sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc} ]
        \big)
        + \Expectation\big[ \Variance( \midPriceOf{\hawOne}_t \mid
        \sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc} ) \big]$.%
        \footnote{%
         Statement \emph{iv.} is the law of total variance given the path,
         and it needs a conditioning event
         that is $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$-measurable.
         With orders of one share,
         the part of $G$ that excludes an empty side is:
         the sides of $\hawOne$ are \eqref{eq.sideSize},
         and the book $\cks$ is a function of the path.
         The part that excludes the floor depends on the offsets, and is not;
         statement \emph{iv.} holds exactly with $G$ replaced by its first part.
         With random sizes the emptying of a side of $\hawQ$ depends on the sizes,
         and the split for $\hawQ$ is a statement on the draws that keep its sides
         occupied.}
 \end{enumerate}
 Write $\partingTime^{\hawQ}$ and $\discrepancy^{\hawQ}_t$ for the parting time and the
 discrepancy of the books $\cks$ and $\hawQ$.
 The four statements hold for them, with $\hawQ$ in place of $\hawOne$.
\end{prop}

We call the two terms of \emph{iv.} the \emph{flow part} and the \emph{placement part}
of the variance:
the variance of what the path alone decides,
and the variance that the draws of $\hawOne$ add given the path.

\begin{proof}
 \emph{i.} is the definition of $\partingTime$.

 \emph{ii.}
 The reflection $p \mapsto 2\midPrice_0 - p$ maps the tick grid to itself,
 since $2\midPrice_0 = \bestBidPrice_0 + \bestAskPrice_0$,
 exchanges the bid and the ask,
 and maps the opening book of Definition \ref{def.threeBooks} to itself.
 On $G$,
 every message of the path and of its mirror is that of
 Example \ref{example.idealisedPlacement}
 or of parts \emph{i.} to \emph{iii.} of Definition \ref{def.geometricPlacement},
 neither the floor nor the price of an empty side being used,
 and these commute with the reflection, the type being replaced by its mirror:
 the rules of Example \ref{example.idealisedPlacement} are the same on the two sides;
 a limit buy at $\bestAskPrice - \tickSizeOfLOB(1+\priceOffset_n)$
 reflects to a limit sell at the reflected best bid plus $\tickSizeOfLOB(1+\priceOffset_n)$;
 and a withdrawal that takes the first level of its side,
 counted from the touch,
 at which the cumulated size exceeds $U_n$ times the size of the side,
 reflects to the withdrawal of the other side that the same $U_n$ defines,
 since the reflection maps the levels of one side, counted from its touch,
 to those of the other, counted from its touch.
 By induction on the events,
 on $G$ the books folded from the mirror are the reflections of those folded from the path,
 so that the discrepancy of the mirror is $-\discrepancy_t$.
 By \eqref{eq.directionSymmetry} the mirror has intensity
 $\swapMatrix\intensity[ ] = \baseIntensity + \excitation\swapMatrix\decayedCounts$,
 so it is a Hawkes process with the specification of the path, with no past,
 and it has the law of the path;
 the draws $(\priceOffset_n, U_n, \orderSize_n)$ are independent of both.
 The exchange of the path with its mirror therefore preserves the law of the path and of
 its draws,
 and it leaves $G$ invariant,
 so that on $G$ the law of $-\discrepancy_t$ is that of $\discrepancy_t$.

 \emph{iii.}
 By \emph{i.} and \emph{ii.},
 $\Variance\,\discrepancy_t = \Expectation\,\discrepancy_t^{2}
 = \Expectation[\discrepancy_t^{2} ; \, \partingTime \leq t]$,
 and $\discrepancy_t \neq 0$ implies $\vert \discrepancy_t \vert \geq \tickSizeOfLOB/2$.

 \emph{iv.} is the law of total variance:
 the book $\cks$ is $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$-measurable,
 so that
 $\Variance(\discrepancy_t \mid \sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc})
 = \Variance(\midPriceOf{\hawOne}_t \mid \sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc})$.

 None of the four arguments uses the size of the orders,
 and the reflection leaves the sizes unchanged,
 so they hold for $\hawQ$.
\end{proof}

The discrepancy is decomposed by an identity that holds on any book.
For a quantity $X$ read after each event write
$\Delta_n X := X_{\arrivalTimes[]_n} - X_{\arrivalTimes[]_{n-1}}$.

\begin{defi}\label{def.outerSize}
 Let both sides of a book be occupied before and after its $n$-th event.
 The \emph{outer sizes} of the event are
 \begin{equation*}
  \outerSize^{b}_n :=
  \begin{cases}
   \nthBestBidSize[1]_{\arrivalTimes[]_{n-1}} & \text{if } \Delta_n \bestBidPrice > 0, \\
   \nthBestBidSize[1]_{\arrivalTimes[]_{n}} & \text{if } \Delta_n \bestBidPrice < 0, \\
   0 & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
  \qquad
  \outerSize^{a}_n :=
  \begin{cases}
   \nthBestAskSize[1]_{\arrivalTimes[]_{n}} & \text{if } \Delta_n \bestAskPrice > 0, \\
   \nthBestAskSize[1]_{\arrivalTimes[]_{n-1}} & \text{if } \Delta_n \bestAskPrice < 0, \\
   0 & \text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
 \end{equation*}
 When a best price moves,
 its outer size is the size at the outer of its two values
 --- the lower of the two best bids, the higher of the two best asks ---
 read on the book in which that value is best.
\end{defi}

\begin{prop}\label{prop.touchIdentity}
 Let both sides of a book be occupied before and after its $n$-th event,
 and write $\orderFlowContribution = \orderFlowContribution^{b} + \orderFlowContribution^{a}$,
 with $\orderFlowContribution^{b}$ the first two and $\orderFlowContribution^{a}$ the last
 two terms of \eqref{eq.orderFlowContribution}.
 Then
 \begin{equation}\label{eq.touchIdentityOneEvent}
  \orderFlowContribution^{b} - \Delta_n \nthBestBidSize[1]
  = \signum(\Delta_n \bestBidPrice) \, \outerSize^{b}_n ,
  \qquad
  \orderFlowContribution^{a} + \Delta_n \nthBestAskSize[1]
  = \signum(\Delta_n \bestAskPrice) \, \outerSize^{a}_n .
 \end{equation}
 Consequently, over a window $(t-\ofiWindow, t]$ on which both sides stay occupied,
 \begin{equation}\label{eq.touchIdentity}
  \OFI_{t,\ofiWindow}
  - \big( \nthBestBidSize[1]_{t} - \nthBestBidSize[1]_{t-\ofiWindow} \big)
  + \big( \nthBestAskSize[1]_{t} - \nthBestAskSize[1]_{t-\ofiWindow} \big)
  = \sum_{n \, : \, t-\ofiWindow < \arrivalTimes[]_n \leq t}
  \big( \signum(\Delta_n \bestBidPrice) \, \outerSize^{b}_n
  + \signum(\Delta_n \bestAskPrice) \, \outerSize^{a}_n \big) .
 \end{equation}
\end{prop}
\begin{proof}
 If the best bid does not move,
 both bid indicators of \eqref{eq.orderFlowContribution} are one,
 $\orderFlowContribution^{b} = \Delta_n \nthBestBidSize[1]$,
 and both sides of the first identity vanish.
 If it rises, only the first is one:
 $\orderFlowContribution^{b} = \nthBestBidSize[1]_{\arrivalTimes[]_n}$,
 and the left-hand side is $\nthBestBidSize[1]_{\arrivalTimes[]_{n-1}} = \outerSize^{b}_n$.
 If it falls, only the second is one:
 $\orderFlowContribution^{b} = -\nthBestBidSize[1]_{\arrivalTimes[]_{n-1}}$,
 and the left-hand side is $-\nthBestBidSize[1]_{\arrivalTimes[]_n} = -\outerSize^{b}_n$.
 The ask is the same mutatis mutandis,
 and summing over the window telescopes the sizes.
\end{proof}

The identity uses Definition \ref{def.orderFlowImbalance}
and no property of the mechanism that produced the book.
When the best bid moves,
$\signum(\Delta_n \bestBidPrice)\,\outerSize^{b}_n
= \big( \tickSizeOfLOB \, \outerSize^{b}_n / \abs{\Delta_n \bestBidPrice} \big)
\, \Delta_n \bestBidPrice / \tickSizeOfLOB$,
so the right-hand side of \eqref{eq.touchIdentity} sums the moves of the two best prices in
ticks,
each weighted by its outer size per tick moved.
Under Assumption \ref{assumption.idealisedBook} that weight is $\uniformDepth$ at every
move.

\begin{corol}\label{corol.touchIdentityUnderTheAssumption}
 Under Assumption \ref{assumption.idealisedBook},
 every move of a best price is by one tick and has outer size $\uniformDepth$,
 and \eqref{eq.touchIdentity} is \eqref{eq.idealisedUpdate}
 with the residual \eqref{eq.idealisedResidual}.
\end{corol}
\begin{proof}
 That a best price moves only by one tick, onto or off a queue of $\uniformDepth$,
 follows from parts \emph{ii.} to \emph{iv.} of the assumption,
 as in Exercise \ref{exercise.idealisedUpdate}.
 The right-hand side of \eqref{eq.touchIdentity} is then
 $\uniformDepth \big( \bestBidPrice_t - \bestBidPrice_{t-\ofiWindow}
 + \bestAskPrice_t - \bestAskPrice_{t-\ofiWindow} \big) / \tickSizeOfLOB
 = 2\uniformDepth \, \Delta\midPrice_{t,\ofiWindow} / \tickSizeOfLOB$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lemma.unitContribution}
 Let $\behindSet$ be the set of the indices $n$
 for which the $n$-th event is a limit order or a withdrawal that the book
 $\hawOne$ places behind the touch of its side.
 Then the contribution \eqref{eq.orderFlowContribution} of the $n$-th event to the
 imbalance of $\hawOne$ is
 $\orderFlowContribution^{\hawOne} = \pressure_{\event[n]} \indicator{n \notin \behindSet}$,
 and
 \begin{equation*}
  \OFI^{\hawOne}_{t,t}
  = \pressure^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc(t)
  - \sum_{n \in \behindSet \, : \, \arrivalTimes[]_n \leq t} \pressure_{\event[n]} ,
  \qquad
  \OFI^{\cks}_{t,t} = \pressure^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc(t) .
 \end{equation*}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
 We show more:
 if the $n$-th event of a geometric book carries the size $\orderSize$,
 and $\behindSet$ is read on that book,
 the contribution of the event is
 \begin{equation}\label{eq.geometricContribution}
  \orderFlowContribution
  = \pressure_{\event[n]} \indicator{n \notin \behindSet} \, \hat{\orderSize}_n ,
 \end{equation}
 where $\hat{\orderSize}_n := \orderSize \wedge \size$ for a market order or a withdrawal
 at the touch,
 $\size$ being the size before the event at the touch it acts on,
 and $\hat{\orderSize}_n := \orderSize$ otherwise.
 With $\orderSize = 1$ and $\size \geq 1$ this is the claim.
 A market buy changes only the ask.
 If $\orderSize < \size$ the best ask stays,
 and $\orderFlowContribution^{a} = -\Delta_n \nthBestAskSize[1] = \orderSize$;
 otherwise it rises,
 and $\orderFlowContribution^{a} = \nthBestAskSize[1]_{\arrivalTimes[]_{n-1}} = \size$,
 however far the order walks the book.
 A limit buy rests in full on the bid, by \eqref{eq.limitPrice}.
 Behind the best bid it changes neither best price nor the size at either,
 and $\orderFlowContribution = 0$;
 at the best bid, $\orderFlowContribution^{b} = \Delta_n \nthBestBidSize[1] = \orderSize$;
 above it, the bid rises and
 $\orderFlowContribution^{b} = \nthBestBidSize[1]_{\arrivalTimes[]_n} = \orderSize$.
 A withdrawal from the bid removes
 $\orderSize \wedge \size^{b}_{\arrivalTimes[]_n-}(p_n)$ at the price $p_n$ it draws,
 and changes only the bid.
 Behind the best bid it changes neither best price nor the size at either,
 and $\orderFlowContribution = 0$.
 At the best bid,
 either it removes less than the size there,
 the bid stays and
 $\orderFlowContribution^{b} = \Delta_n \nthBestBidSize[1] = -(\orderSize \wedge \size)$;
 or the queue empties, the bid falls to the next occupied price,
 and $\orderFlowContribution^{b} = -\nthBestBidSize[1]_{\arrivalTimes[]_{n-1}}
 = -(\orderSize \wedge \size)$.
 The sell types are the same mutatis mutandis,
 and in each case the sign is $\pressure_{\event[n]}$.
 For the book $\cks$, Proposition \ref{prop.signedSizeContribution} gives
 $\orderFlowContribution^{\cks} = \pressure_{\event[n]}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{prop}\label{prop.discrepancyDecomposition}
 For every $t$,
 \begin{equation}\label{eq.discrepancyDecomposition}
 \begin{split}
  \frac{2\uniformDepth}{\tickSizeOfLOB} \, \discrepancy_t
  =\;&
  \sum_{n \in \behindSet \, : \, \arrivalTimes[]_n \leq t} \pressure_{\event[n]}
  - \Big[ \big( \nthBestBidSize[1,\cks]_t - \nthBestBidSize[1,\hawOne]_t \big)
  - \big( \nthBestAskSize[1,\cks]_t - \nthBestAskSize[1,\hawOne]_t \big) \Big]
  \\
  &+ \sum_{n \, : \, \arrivalTimes[]_n \leq t}
  \Big[ \signum(\Delta_n \nthBestBidPrice[1,\hawOne])
  \Big( \outerSize^{b,\hawOne}_n
  - \uniformDepth \, \frac{\abs{\Delta_n \nthBestBidPrice[1,\hawOne]}}{\tickSizeOfLOB} \Big)
  \\
  &\hphantom{+ \sum_{n \, : \, \arrivalTimes[]_n \leq t} \Big[}
  + \signum(\Delta_n \nthBestAskPrice[1,\hawOne])
  \Big( \outerSize^{a,\hawOne}_n
  - \uniformDepth \, \frac{\abs{\Delta_n \nthBestAskPrice[1,\hawOne]}}{\tickSizeOfLOB} \Big)
  \Big] .
 \end{split}
 \end{equation}
\end{prop}
\begin{proof}
 For the book $\cks$, Proposition \ref{prop.idealisedUpdate} over $(0, t]$,
 with Lemma \ref{lemma.unitContribution} and the opening touches of $\uniformDepth$, gives
 \begin{equation*}
  \frac{2\uniformDepth}{\tickSizeOfLOB} \big( \midPriceOf{\cks}_t - \midPrice_0 \big)
  = \pressure^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc(t)
  - \big( \nthBestBidSize[1,\cks]_t - \uniformDepth \big)
  + \big( \nthBestAskSize[1,\cks]_t - \uniformDepth \big) .
 \end{equation*}
 For the book $\hawOne$,
 write \eqref{eq.touchIdentity} over $(0, t]$,
 and add and subtract
 $\uniformDepth \abs{\Delta_n \nthBestBidPrice[1,\hawOne]} / \tickSizeOfLOB$
 and its ask counterpart inside each signed term:
 the signed sum of these is
 $2\uniformDepth \big( \midPriceOf{\hawOne}_t - \midPrice_0 \big) / \tickSizeOfLOB$,
 so that
 \begin{equation*}
  \frac{2\uniformDepth}{\tickSizeOfLOB} \big( \midPriceOf{\hawOne}_t - \midPrice_0 \big)
  = \OFI^{\hawOne}_{t,t}
  - \big( \nthBestBidSize[1,\hawOne]_t - \uniformDepth \big)
  + \big( \nthBestAskSize[1,\hawOne]_t - \uniformDepth \big)
  - \sum_{n \, : \, \arrivalTimes[]_n \leq t} \big[ \cdots \big] ,
 \end{equation*}
 the bracket being that of \eqref{eq.discrepancyDecomposition}.
 Subtract, and use Lemma \ref{lemma.unitContribution} for the imbalance of $\hawOne$.
\end{proof}

The three terms of \eqref{eq.discrepancyDecomposition} are
the events that $\hawOne$ places behind its touches,
the mismatch of the touches at $t$,
and the mismatch of the depth through which $\hawOne$ moves.
The second is bounded when the touches of $\hawOne$ are,
those of $\cks$ lying between one share and $\uniformDepth$.
The third vanishes on every move of $\hawOne$ by one tick onto or off a queue of
$\uniformDepth$.
The first is a sum over the draws of $\hawOne$,
and its mean given the path is read off the books before each event.

\begin{prop}\label{prop.placementMartingale}
 Let
 $\behindProbability_n := \Prob\big( n \in \behindSet \mid \sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc},
 \text{the draws of the events before the } n\text{-th} \big)$.
 Then
 $\behindProbability_n = (1-\offsetParameter)^{k_n}$ for a limit order,
 with $k_n := \LOBspread^{\hawOne}_{\arrivalTimes[]_n-}/\tickSizeOfLOB$
 the spread of $\hawOne$ before the event,
 $\behindProbability_n = 1 - \size^{\hawOne}/\size^{\hawOne}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$ for a
 withdrawal,
 with $\size^{\hawOne}$ the size at the touch of its side
 and $\size^{\hawOne}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$ the size on that side, before the event,
 and $\behindProbability_n = 0$ for a market order.
 The process
 \begin{equation*}
  \martingale^{\behindSet}_t
  := \sum_{n \, : \, \arrivalTimes[]_n \leq t}
  \pressure_{\event[n]} \big( \indicator{n \in \behindSet} - \behindProbability_n \big)
 \end{equation*}
 satisfies
 $\Expectation[ \martingale^{\behindSet}_t \mid \sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc} ] = 0$ and
 $\Expectation[ (\martingale^{\behindSet}_t)^{2} \mid \sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc} ]
 = \Expectation\big[ \sum_{n \, : \, \arrivalTimes[]_n \leq t}
 \behindProbability_n (1 - \behindProbability_n)
 \,\big\vert\, \sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc} \big]$,
 and
 \begin{equation*}
  \OFI^{\hawOne}_{t,t}
  = \pressure^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc(t)
  - \sum_{n \, : \, \arrivalTimes[]_n \leq t} \pressure_{\event[n]} \behindProbability_n
  - \martingale^{\behindSet}_t .
 \end{equation*}
\end{prop}
\begin{proof}
 By \eqref{eq.limitPrice} a limit order at a spread of $k$ ticks lands behind its touch
 exactly when $\priceOffset_n \geq k$,
 which has probability $(1-\offsetParameter)^{k}$ by \eqref{eq.priceOffset}.
 A withdrawal lands behind its touch exactly when it does not draw it,
 and it draws it with probability $\size^{\hawOne}/\size^{\hawOne}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$.
 Let $\mathcal{G}_n$ be generated by $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$
 and the draws of the first $n$ events.
 Then $\behindProbability_n$ is $\mathcal{G}_{n-1}$-measurable,
 and the offset and the level of the $n$-th event are drawn,
 given $\mathcal{G}_{n-1}$, on the book that the earlier events left.
 The partial sums of
 $\pressure_{\event[n]}(\indicator{n \in \behindSet} - \behindProbability_n)$
 therefore form a martingale in $(\mathcal{G}_n)_n$,
 with increments bounded by one
 and of conditional variance $\behindProbability_n(1 - \behindProbability_n)$,
 since $\pressure_e^{2} = 1$;
 its increments are orthogonal,
 and the number of events of $(0, t]$ is $\mathcal{G}_0$-measurable.
 The identity is Lemma \ref{lemma.unitContribution}.
\end{proof}

The imbalance of $\hawOne$ is the signed count,
less what the books of $\hawOne$ predict it places behind its touches,
less a noise of the placement alone,
whose variance given the path is known exactly.

Propositions \ref{prop.discrepancyDecomposition} and \ref{prop.placementMartingale} are
exact, and they do not give the variance in closed form.
Two approximations do, one for each regime of Corollary \ref{corol.twoRegimes}.
In the second regime,
on horizons short against the life of a side,
a best price of $\hawOne$ moves only by a large deviation of its touch,
by Approximation \ref{approx.secondRegime}.

\begin{approximation}\label{approx.secondRegimeVariance}
 Let $\offsetParameter\stationaryIntensity[3] > \stationaryIntensity[2]$,
 and let $t$ be short against
 $(L\uniformDepth)^{2} / (\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\countCovarianceRate\,\sideVector{b}$.
 On $\lbrace \firstMove^{\hawOne} > t \rbrace$,
 $\discrepancy_t = \midPriceOf{\cks}_t - \midPrice_0$,
 which is $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$-measurable,
 and
 \begin{equation*}
  \Variance\Big( \frac{\discrepancy_t}{\tickSizeOfLOB} \Big)
  = \frac{\pressure^{\transpose}\countCovarianceRate\,\pressure}{(2\uniformDepth)^{2}} \, t
  + O(\sqrt{t})
  + O\big( t \, \Prob(\firstMove^{\hawOne} \leq t)^{1/2} \big) ,
 \end{equation*}
 the last term bounding the placement part.
\end{approximation}
\begin{proof}
 On $\lbrace \firstMove^{\hawOne} > t \rbrace$
 the mid-price of $\hawOne$ stays at $\midPrice_0$.
 By Proposition \ref{prop.idealisedUpdate} and Lemma \ref{lemma.unitContribution},
 $(\midPriceOf{\cks}_t - \midPrice_0)/\tickSizeOfLOB
 = \pressure^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc(t)/(2\uniformDepth)
 + \residual^{\cks}_{t,t}/\tickSizeOfLOB$,
 where by \eqref{eq.idealisedResidual}
 $\vert \residual^{\cks}_{t,t}/\tickSizeOfLOB \vert \leq (\uniformDepth - 1)/(2\uniformDepth)$,
 the touches of $\cks$ lying between one share and $\uniformDepth$.
 By \eqref{eq.countsFromNoPast},
 $\pressure^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc(t)$ is
 $\pressure^{\transpose}(I - \branchingMatrix)^{-1}\martingale(t)$
 up to a term bounded in mean square, since $\pressure^{\transpose}\stationaryIntensity = 0$.
 The components of $\martingale$ have no common jumps, by \eqref{eq.noCommonJumps},
 and $\Expectation\,\martingale_e(t)^{2} = \Expectation\,\compensator_e(t)
 = t\,\stationaryIntensity[e] + O(1)$,
 the expected intensity converging exponentially fast from no past;
 so the martingale term has variance $t\,\pressure^{\transpose}\countCovarianceRate\,\pressure
 + O(1)$, by \eqref{eq.flowVolatility}.
 The bounded terms contribute $O(\sqrt{t})$, by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality.
 The last term bounds
 $\Expectation\big[ \discrepancy_t^{2} - (\midPriceOf{\cks}_t - \midPrice_0)^{2} ;
 \, \firstMove^{\hawOne} \leq t \big] / \tickSizeOfLOB^{2}$
 and the placement part
 $\Expectation\big[ \Variance( \midPriceOf{\hawOne}_t \mid \sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc} ) \big]
 \leq \Expectation\big[ (\midPriceOf{\hawOne}_t - \midPrice_0)^{2} ;
 \, \firstMove^{\hawOne} \leq t \big]$,
 by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality,
 the fourth moments of the mid-prices less $\midPrice_0$ being $O(t^{2})$.
\end{proof}

In the first regime the best prices of $\hawOne$ move,
and a closed form needs two hypotheses on its books:
\begin{enumerate}[label={\emph{H\arabic*}.}]
 \item \emph{constant placement}:
       $\behindProbability_n = \behindProbability_{\event[n]}$
       for a vector $\behindProbability \in [0,1]^{\numEventTypes}$;
 \item \emph{fixed depth}: there is a constant $\effectiveDepth > 0$ such that
       \begin{equation*}
        \frac{2\effectiveDepth}{\tickSizeOfLOB}
        \big( \midPriceOf{\hawOne}_t - \midPrice_0 \big)
        = \OFI^{\hawOne}_{t,t} + R_t ,
       \end{equation*}
       with $\Expectation R_t^{2}$ bounded in $t$.
\end{enumerate}
By \eqref{eq.touchIdentity} over $(0, t]$,
$-R_t$ is the sum of
$(\nthBestBidSize[1,\hawOne]_t - \uniformDepth) - (\nthBestAskSize[1,\hawOne]_t - \uniformDepth)$
and of the last sum of \eqref{eq.discrepancyDecomposition} with $\effectiveDepth$ in place
of $\uniformDepth$:
H2 says that $\hawOne$ moves as if it held $\effectiveDepth$ on every tick it crosses,
up to an error bounded in mean square.
When the variance of $\midPriceOf{\hawOne}_t$ grows without bound,
H2 and the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality identify the depth:
\begin{equation}\label{eq.effectiveDepth}
 \effectiveDepth
 = \lim_{t \to \infty} \frac{\tickSizeOfLOB}{2} \,
 \frac{\CoVariance\big( \OFI^{\hawOne}_{t,t}, \midPriceOf{\hawOne}_t \big)}
 {\Variance\,\midPriceOf{\hawOne}_t} ,
\end{equation}
the slope of the regression of the imbalance of $\hawOne$ on its mid-price,
counted in half ticks.
When the touches of $\hawOne$ are bounded in mean square,
\eqref{eq.touchIdentity} allows the imbalance to be replaced
by the sum of the outer sizes of the moves, signed by the moves.
Write $\circ$ for the entrywise product,
$\mathbf{1}$ for the vector of ones,
and let
\begin{equation*}
 c := \frac{\mathbf{1}}{2\uniformDepth}
 - \frac{\mathbf{1} - \behindProbability}{2\effectiveDepth} .
\end{equation*}
An event of type $e$ moves the mid-price of $\cks$ by $\pressure_e/(2\uniformDepth)$ ticks,
and under H1 and H2 that of $\hawOne$ by $\pressure_e(1 - \behindProbability_e)/(2\effectiveDepth)$
in mean:
$c_e$ is the difference of the two impacts.

\begin{approximation}\label{approx.firstRegimeVariance}
 Let H1 and H2 hold, and write $O(1)$ for a term bounded in mean square uniformly in $t$.
 Then
 \begin{equation*}
  \frac{\discrepancy_t}{\tickSizeOfLOB}
  = (\pressure \circ c)^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc(t)
  + \frac{\martingale^{\behindSet}_t}{2\effectiveDepth} + O(1) ,
  \qquad
  \Expectation\Big[ \frac{\discrepancy_t}{\tickSizeOfLOB}
  \,\Big\vert\, \sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc} \Big]
  = (\pressure \circ c)^{\transpose}
  \big( \multiCountingProc(t) - t\,\stationaryIntensity \big) + O(1) ,
 \end{equation*}
 and
 \begin{equation*}
  \Variance\Big( \frac{\discrepancy_t}{\tickSizeOfLOB} \Big)
  = t \, (\pressure \circ c)^{\transpose} \countCovarianceRate \, (\pressure \circ c)
  + t \sum_{e} \frac{\behindProbability_e (1 - \behindProbability_e)
  \stationaryIntensity[e]}{(2\effectiveDepth)^{2}}
  + O(\sqrt{t}) ,
 \end{equation*}
 the first term being the flow part and the second the placement part.
\end{approximation}
\begin{proof}
 Under H1,
 $\sum_{n \, : \, \arrivalTimes[]_n \leq t} \pressure_{\event[n]} \behindProbability_n
 = (\pressure \circ \behindProbability)^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc(t)$,
 so that by H2 and Proposition \ref{prop.placementMartingale}
 \begin{equation*}
  \frac{\midPriceOf{\hawOne}_t - \midPrice_0}{\tickSizeOfLOB}
  = \frac{(\pressure \circ (\mathbf{1} - \behindProbability))^{\transpose}
  \multiCountingProc(t)}{2\effectiveDepth}
  - \frac{\martingale^{\behindSet}_t}{2\effectiveDepth}
  + \frac{R_t}{2\effectiveDepth} .
 \end{equation*}
 The book $\cks$ gives
 $(\midPriceOf{\cks}_t - \midPrice_0)/\tickSizeOfLOB
 = \pressure^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc(t)/(2\uniformDepth) + O(1)$,
 as in the proof of Approximation \ref{approx.secondRegimeVariance},
 and subtracting gives the first identity.
 The exchange of the path with its mirror,
 of the proof of Proposition \ref{prop.discrepancyMoments},
 maps an event of type $e$ placed behind its touch
 to an event of type $\swapMatrix(e)$ placed behind its touch,
 on the reflected book,
 and preserves the law of the path and of its draws;
 under H1, therefore,
 $\behindProbability_{\swapMatrix(e)} = \behindProbability_{e}$.
 The entries of $c$ are then equal on the two members of each pair,
 while those of $\pressure$ have opposite signs,
 so that $(\pressure \circ c)^{\transpose}\stationaryIntensity = 0$
 by $\swapMatrix\stationaryIntensity = \stationaryIntensity$;
 with $\Expectation[\martingale^{\behindSet}_t \mid \sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}] = 0$
 this gives the second.
 For the variance,
 $\Variance\big( (\pressure \circ c)^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc(t) \big)
 = t \, (\pressure \circ c)^{\transpose} \countCovarianceRate \, (\pressure \circ c) + O(\sqrt{t})$
 as in the proof of Approximation \ref{approx.secondRegimeVariance};
 $\Variance\,\martingale^{\behindSet}_t
 = \sum_{e} \behindProbability_e (1 - \behindProbability_e) \Expectation\,\countingProc(t)
 = t \sum_{e} \behindProbability_e (1 - \behindProbability_e) \stationaryIntensity[e] + O(1)$
 by Proposition \ref{prop.placementMartingale};
 the two are uncorrelated,
 $\martingale^{\behindSet}_t$ having mean zero given $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$;
 and the bounded terms contribute $O(\sqrt{t})$ by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality.
 The conditional mean is $(\pressure \circ c)^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc(t)$
 up to a term bounded in mean square,
 so its variance is the first term,
 and the second is the rest.
\end{proof}

Given the path,
the expected discrepancy is the signed count weighted, type by type,
by the difference $c_e$ of the impacts of one event on the two mid-prices.
As $\effectiveDepth \to \infty$ the impact on $\hawOne$ and the placement part vanish,
and Approximation \ref{approx.firstRegimeVariance} becomes
Approximation \ref{approx.secondRegimeVariance}.
Under H1,
$\behindProbability_e$ is the probability that an event of type $e$ is placed behind its
touch:
zero for a market order,
$(1-\offsetParameter)^{k}$ for a limit order at a spread of $k$ ticks,
and for a withdrawal one minus the share of the touch in the size of its side,
which by \eqref{eq.sideSize} is close to one when $L$ is large.
The depth $\effectiveDepth$ is the slope \eqref{eq.effectiveDepth};
the mean outer size per tick crossed at the moves of $\hawOne$
is a different statistic,
and the two need not agree.
Approximation \ref{approx.firstRegimeVariance} is a first-order description of the first
regime:
H1 and H2 hold on the books of $\hawOne$ only approximately,
and the split of the variance into its flow and placement parts is qualitative.

\subsubsection{Uncertain sizes}
\label{sec.uncertainSizes}

We now set the book $\cks$ against $\hawQ$,
whose sizes we count in units of $\meanSize$:
its opening levels hold $\uniformDepth$ units,
and its $n$-th order carries $\xi_n := \orderSize_n / \meanSize$ units,
with $\Expectation\,\xi_n = 1$.
The statements of Sections \ref{sec.partingTime} and \ref{sec.discrepancy}
are read for $\cks$ against $\hawQ$ item by item,
and we list what changes.

Lemma \ref{lemma.oneBestPrice} and Corollary \ref{corol.partingAtOneQuote}
hold with $\hawQ$ in place of $\hawOne$, with the same proofs:
a market buy walks the ask, and moves only it, up.
In Proposition \ref{prop.partingProbability} the rows of the limit orders are unchanged,
since the price of a limit order does not depend on its size,
and those of the market orders and of the withdrawals change.

\begin{prop}\label{prop.partingProbabilitySizes}
 In Proposition \ref{prop.partingProbability}, with $\hawQ$ in place of $\hawOne$,
 the book $\hawQ$ moves the touch of the side of the event
 \begin{enumerate}[label={\emph{\roman{*}}.}]
  \item for a market order,
        exactly when $\xi_n \geq \size^{\hawQ}$,
        which has probability $\Prob(\xi_n \geq \size^{\hawQ})$,
        to the first price at which the size of the side, cumulated from the touch,
        exceeds $\xi_n$,
        possibly several occupied levels away;
  \item for a withdrawal,
        exactly when it draws the touch and $\xi_n \geq \size^{\hawQ}$,
        which has probability
        $\Prob(\xi_n \geq \size^{\hawQ}) \, \size^{\hawQ} / \size^{\hawQ}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$,
        by $g$ ticks.
 \end{enumerate}
 If the law of $\xi_n$ has unbounded support, as the lognormal law has,
 these probabilities are positive at every touch,
 and the books $\cks$ and $\hawQ$ are never quiet.
\end{prop}
\begin{proof}
 A market buy of $\xi_n$ units is matched against the ask in price-time priority,
 by the matching rule of Section \ref{sec.orderDrivenMarkets},
 and consumes the levels from the best ask until its size is exhausted:
 the best ask moves exactly when $\xi_n \geq \size^{\hawQ}$,
 to the first price at which the cumulated size exceeds $\xi_n$.
 A withdrawal cancels $\xi_n \wedge \size^{\hawQ}_{\arrivalTimes[]_n-}(p_n)$ at the price
 it draws,
 by Definition \ref{def.geometricPlacement}~\emph{iii.},
 and moves the touch exactly when it draws the touch and $\xi_n \geq \size^{\hawQ}$,
 to the next occupied price.
 The size $\xi_n$ is independent of $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$,
 of the earlier draws and of the level drawn,
 and $\Prob(\xi_n \geq \size) > 0$ for every $\size$ when its support is unbounded.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{remark.fluidScaleWithSizes}
 In \eqref{eq.hawkesTouch} for $\hawQ$,
 a limit order that joins the touch brings $\xi_n$ units,
 and a market order or a withdrawal at the touch removes $\xi_n \wedge \size^{\hawQ}$.
 With $\Expectation\,\xi_n = 1$ and $\Variance\,\xi_n < \infty$,
 the three steps of the proof of Proposition \ref{prop.fluidTouch} go through
 with $\xi_n$ in the martingale of step 2,
 except for two truncations:
 at the touch, which acts only when the touch holds a number of units of order one,
 and of the withdrawals behind the touch from levels holding fewer than $\xi_n$ units,
 which changes the size of a side by an amount that the argument does not control.
 To first order, therefore,
 the equation \eqref{eq.fluidTouch}, the time $\fluidEmptying$ and the two regimes of
 Corollary \ref{corol.twoRegimes} are unchanged,
 the first move of $\hawQ$ coming when its touch holds a number of units of order one
 rather than none.
\end{remark}

The mean of $\discrepancy^{\hawQ}_t$ is zero,
and its variance splits into a flow part and a placement part,
by Proposition \ref{prop.discrepancyMoments}.
Its decomposition gains one term.

\begin{prop}\label{prop.discrepancyDecompositionSizes}
 Let $\behindSet$ be as in Lemma \ref{lemma.unitContribution}, read on $\hawQ$,
 and let $\hat{\xi}_n := \xi_n \wedge \size^{\hawQ}$
 for a market order or a withdrawal at the touch,
 $\size^{\hawQ}$ being the size before the event at the touch it acts on,
 and $\hat{\xi}_n := \xi_n$ otherwise.
 Then $2\uniformDepth\,\discrepancy^{\hawQ}_t / \tickSizeOfLOB$ is the right-hand side of
 \eqref{eq.discrepancyDecomposition} read on $\hawQ$, with sizes in units,
 plus
 \begin{equation*}
  \sum_{n \, : \, \arrivalTimes[]_n \leq t}
  \pressure_{\event[n]} \big( 1 - \hat{\xi}_n \big) \indicator{n \notin \behindSet} .
 \end{equation*}
\end{prop}
\begin{proof}
 The proof of Proposition \ref{prop.discrepancyDecomposition},
 with \eqref{eq.geometricContribution} in units in place of Lemma \ref{lemma.unitContribution}:
 $\orderFlowContribution^{\hawQ}/\meanSize
 = \pressure_{\event[n]} \hat{\xi}_n \indicator{n \notin \behindSet}$,
 and $\pressure_{\event[n]} - \pressure_{\event[n]} \hat{\xi}_n \indicator{n \notin \behindSet}
 = \pressure_{\event[n]} \indicator{n \in \behindSet}
 + \pressure_{\event[n]} (1 - \hat{\xi}_n) \indicator{n \notin \behindSet}$.
\end{proof}

The new term is the cost of the sizes,
and it vanishes when every order carries one unit.
Together with the first term of \eqref{eq.discrepancyDecomposition}
it is the sum of
$\pressure_{\event[n]} \big( 1 - \hat{\xi}_n \indicator{n \notin \behindSet} \big)$.
Given $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$ and the draws of the earlier events,
$\xi_n$ is independent of the event $\lbrace n \in \behindSet \rbrace$,
with mean one,
and that event has the probability $\behindProbability_n$, read on the books of $\hawQ$.
Where $\hat{\xi}_n = \xi_n$,
as for every limit order and for every event acting on a touch of more than $\xi_n$ units,
the increment
$\pressure_{\event[n]} \big( 1 - \xi_n \indicator{n \notin \behindSet}
- \behindProbability_n \big)$
therefore has conditional mean zero and conditional variance
\begin{equation}\label{eq.sizePlacementVariance}
 \Variance\big( \xi_n \indicator{n \notin \behindSet} \big)
 = \behindProbability_n (1 - \behindProbability_n)
 + (1 - \behindProbability_n) \, \Variance\,\xi_n ,
\end{equation}
the variance being taken given $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$ and the earlier draws.

\begin{remark}\label{remark.whatTheSizesAdd}
 Under H1 and H2 read on $\hawQ$, with $\effectiveDepth$ in units,
 and setting aside the truncation at the touch,
 the proof of Approximation \ref{approx.firstRegimeVariance} with the increments
 \eqref{eq.sizePlacementVariance}
 adds
 $\Variance\,\xi_n \sum_{e} (1 - \behindProbability_e) \stationaryIntensity[e]
 / (2\effectiveDepth)^{2}$
 to the rate of the placement part,
 the market orders entering with $\behindProbability_e = 0$.
 It is neither the whole of what the sizes add to the variance nor a bound on it.
 The truncation is not negligible:
 at each move of $\hawQ$ by a market order or a withdrawal at its touch,
 $\hat{\xi}_n$ is the size of the touch and not $\xi_n$,
 and the sum of $\pressure_{\event[n]} (\xi_n - \hat{\xi}_n)$ over these moves
 has a variance that grows with their number.
 The sizes also make the depth through which $\hawQ$ moves random, through its outer sizes,
 and they make the whole-book imbalance random given the path:
 up to the truncation of the withdrawals,
 the argument of \eqref{eq.sideSize} gives, in units,
 $\size^{b,\hawQ}_t(\tickSizeOfLOB\N) - \size^{a,\hawQ}_t(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)
 = \sum_{n \, : \, \arrivalTimes[]_n \leq t} \pressure_{\event[n]} \xi_n$,
 whose variance given $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$ is
 $\Variance\,\xi_n \, \groundProc(t)$.
 The closed form carries none of the three.
 With random sizes,
 whether an event moves a touch depends on its size more than on the order of the events,
 and part of the variance moves from the flow part to the placement part.
 And the size resting on a side of $\hawQ$,
 which in $\hawOne$ is the walk \eqref{eq.sideSize}
 of variance rate $(\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\countCovarianceRate\,\sideVector{b}$,
 has by the law of total variance the larger rate
 $(\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\countCovarianceRate\,\sideVector{b}
 + \Variance\,\xi_n \, ( \stationaryIntensity[3] + \stationaryIntensity[2]
 + \stationaryIntensity[5] )$
 in units,
 and $\meanSize^{2}$ times it in shares.
\end{remark}
