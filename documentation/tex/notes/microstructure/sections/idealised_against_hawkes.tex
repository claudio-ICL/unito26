Equation \eqref{eq.idealisedUpdate} is exact for the flow of
Example \ref{example.idealisedPlacement}.
We drive that flow and the flow of Definition \ref{def.geometricPlacement}
with the same events from the same book,
and ask how far the identity carries over.
We proceed in two steps.
First we study the time at which the two mid-prices first differ.
Then we study the discrepancy of the mid-prices after that time:
its mean, and what can be said of its variance,
with orders of one share and of random size.

\subsubsection{Three flows on one path}
\label{sec.threeFlows}

\begin{defi}\label{def.threeBooks}
 Let $(\arrivalTimes[]_n, \event[n], \orderSize_n)_{n \geq 1}$ be the order flow of
 Definition \ref{def.orderFlowProcess},
 with no event before time zero,
 and let the \emph{opening book} have a spread of one tick
 and $L$ levels on each side,
 one on every tick,
 its best bid holding $\nthBestBidSize[1]_0$ shares and its best ask $\nthBestAskSize[1]_0$,
 with $1 \leq \nthBestBidSize[1]_0, \nthBestAskSize[1]_0 \leq \uniformDepth$,
 and every other level $\uniformDepth$.
 Three books are folded from the opening book:
 \begin{enumerate}[label={\emph{\roman{*}}.}]
  \item the book $\cks$,
        by the messages that Example \ref{example.idealisedPlacement} makes of
        $(\arrivalTimes[]_n, \event[n])$;
  \item the book $\hawOne$,
        by the messages that Definition \ref{def.geometricPlacement} makes of
        $(\arrivalTimes[]_n, \event[n], 1)$;
  \item the book $\hawQ$,
        by the messages that Definition \ref{def.geometricPlacement} makes of
        $(\arrivalTimes[]_n, \event[n], \orderSize_n)$,
        from the opening book with every size multiplied by $\meanSize$.
 \end{enumerate}
\end{defi}

The opening book satisfies part \emph{iii.} of Assumption \ref{assumption.idealisedBook},
so the book $\cks$ is the book of Example \ref{example.idealisedPlacement}:
our version of the book of \cite{CKS14pri},
along which \eqref{eq.idealisedUpdate} holds.
The books $\hawOne$ and $\hawQ$ are those of the Hawkes flow with the geometric rule for
prices,
with orders of one share and with the sizes of Definition \ref{def.orderFlowProcess},
which are lognormal, of mean $\meanSize$.
We give the three flows of messages the names of their books.
A quantity of a book carries its name as a superscript:
$\midPriceOf{\cks}_t$, $\nthBestBidSize[1,\hawOne]_t$, $\OFI^{\hawQ}_{t,t}$.
As in Definition \ref{def.orderFlowImbalance},
a quantity indexed by $\arrivalTimes[]_n$ is read after the $n$-th event,
and $\arrivalTimes[]_0 := 0$ indexes the opening book.
We write $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$ for the $\sigma$-algebra generated by the
arrival times and the types,
and $\size^{b}_t(\tickSizeOfLOB\N) := \sum_{p} \size^{b}_t(p)$
for the size resting on the whole bid,
and likewise $\size^{a}_t(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$ on the ask.

The placement of Example \ref{example.idealisedPlacement} draws nothing beyond the events,
so the book $\cks$ is $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$-measurable.
The geometric placement draws, for each event, an offset or the level of a withdrawal,
and the orders of $\hawQ$ carry a size besides.
Given $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$ and the draws of the earlier events,
these have the laws of Definitions \ref{def.orderFlowProcess}
and \ref{def.geometricPlacement},
the level of a withdrawal being drawn on the book as the earlier events left it.
We represent that draw by a variable $U_n$,
uniform on $(0,1)$ and independent of the path and of the other draws:
the withdrawal takes the first level of its side,
counted from the touch outwards,
at which the cumulated size exceeds $U_n$ times the size of the side.
This is the draw of Definition \ref{def.geometricPlacement}~\emph{iii.}
Write $\swapMatrix(e)$ for the type with which $\swapMatrix$ exchanges the type $e$.
The \emph{mirror} of the path is the path $(\arrivalTimes[]_n, \swapMatrix(\event[n]))_n$,
with the same $\priceOffset_n$, $U_n$ and $\orderSize_n$.

The books $\cks$ and $\hawOne$ receive the same events, with orders of one share,
and differ only in where the orders go.
The books $\hawOne$ and $\hawQ$ receive the same events and price them by the same rule,
and differ only in the size of the orders.
By Proposition \ref{prop.signedSizeContribution}
the imbalance of the book $\cks$ is the signed count $\pressure^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc$,
so that \eqref{eq.idealisedUpdate} counts events,
and a size other than one acts on it as an uncertainty in the observation of the size of
each order.
Setting $\cks$ against $\hawOne$ therefore isolates the placement of the orders,
and setting it against $\hawQ$ adds the uncertainty in their size.

\begin{assumption}\label{assumption.balancedFlow}
 The specification is direction-symmetric, with $\branchingRatio < 1$,
 the flow is \emph{balanced} on each side:
 \begin{equation*}
  \stationaryIntensity[3] = \stationaryIntensity[2] + \stationaryIntensity[5] ,
  \qquad
  \stationaryIntensity[4] = \stationaryIntensity[1] + \stationaryIntensity[6] ,
 \end{equation*}
 and the opening best bid lies above one tick.
\end{assumption}

By \eqref{eq.directionSymmetry} and \eqref{eq.stationaryIntensity},
$\swapMatrix\stationaryIntensity = \stationaryIntensity$,
so the two conditions of balance are one.

We study the three flows on books that stay well behaved.
Throughout, we work on the event $G$ that,
on the horizon considered,
on the path and on its mirror,
no side of any of the three books is empty
and no limit price meets the floor of Definition \ref{def.geometricPlacement}.
Every statement below is made on $G$,
and expectations and laws are taken on it;
we do not repeat it.
On a book of many levels, priced far above one tick,
the complement of $G$ has small probability,
and by its definition
$G$ is invariant under the exchange of the path with its mirror.

With orders of one share,
a withdrawal from an occupied side removes one share wherever it is drawn.
While the bid of $\hawOne$ is occupied,
a limit buy rests in full on it, by \eqref{eq.limitPrice},
a market sell removes one share from it,
and no other event changes it,
so that, with
$\sideVector{b} := \unitVector_3 - \unitVector_2 - \unitVector_5$
and
$\sideVector{a} := \unitVector_4 - \unitVector_1 - \unitVector_6$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq.sideSize}
 \size^{b,\hawOne}_t(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)
 = \size^{b}_0(\tickSizeOfLOB\N) + (\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc(t) ,
 \qquad
 \size^{a,\hawOne}_t(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)
 = \size^{a}_0(\tickSizeOfLOB\N) + (\sideVector{a})^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc(t) ,
\end{equation}
where $\size^{b}_0(\tickSizeOfLOB\N) = (L-1)\uniformDepth + \nthBestBidSize[1]_0$
and $\size^{a}_0(\tickSizeOfLOB\N) = (L-1)\uniformDepth + \nthBestAskSize[1]_0$
are the sizes of the two sides of the opening book.
The entries of $\sideVector{b}$ are the shares that an event of each type brings to the bid,
those of $\sideVector{a}$ the shares it brings to the ask,
and $\sideVector{b} - \sideVector{a} = \pressure$.
Under balance $(\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\stationaryIntensity = 0$,
so that by \eqref{eq.forwardMeanCountsDeviation} from no past
the mean of $(\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc(t)$ is bounded in $t$,
and by the argument of the proof of Proposition \ref{prop.forecastAtLongHorizons}
its variance grows as $t \, (\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\countCovarianceRate\,\sideVector{b}$.
A side of the opening book,
of more than $(L-1)\uniformDepth$ shares and at most $L\uniformDepth$,
therefore lasts a time of the order of
$(L\uniformDepth)^{2} / (\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\countCovarianceRate\,\sideVector{b}$,
which is $L^{2}$ times the time in which the same walk moves by $\uniformDepth$.

\begin{remark}\label{remark.wholeBookImbalance}
 By \eqref{eq.sideSize} and $\sideVector{b} - \sideVector{a} = \pressure$,
 \begin{equation*}
  \size^{b,\hawOne}_t(\tickSizeOfLOB\N) - \size^{a,\hawOne}_t(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)
  = \nthBestBidSize[1]_0 - \nthBestAskSize[1]_0
  + \pressure^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc(t) :
 \end{equation*}
 the whole-book imbalance of $\hawOne$ is that of the opening touches plus the signed count,
 whatever the offsets and the levels drawn.
 The book $\cks$ has the same whole-book imbalance.
 Every event changes the size on each side of $\cks$ as it changes that of $\hawOne$,
 except a limit order sent to the opposite touch,
 which takes one share from that side and adds none to its own:
 at each such order both sides of $\cks$ fall by one share relative to \eqref{eq.sideSize}.
 The book $\cks$ turns this imbalance into a mid-price at the fixed depth $\uniformDepth$ per
 tick;
 the book $\hawOne$ has no fixed depth.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{The first time the mid-prices differ}
\label{sec.partingTime}

The books $\cks$ and $\hawOne$ open from the same book and receive the same events,
so their mid-prices agree at first.
We ask when they first differ,
and which events make them differ.

\begin{defi}\label{def.partingTime}
 The \emph{parting time} of the books $\cks$ and $\hawOne$ is
 \begin{equation*}
  \partingTime := \inf \big\lbrace t \geq 0 :
  \midPriceOf{\cks}_t \neq \midPriceOf{\hawOne}_t \big\rbrace .
 \end{equation*}
\end{defi}

\begin{lemma}\label{lemma.oneBestPrice}
 In each of the three books an event moves at most one best price,
 up if $\pressure_{\event[n]} = +1$ and down if $\pressure_{\event[n]} = -1$.
 If the best prices of the books $\cks$ and $\hawOne$ agree before an event
 that moves a best price in both,
 it moves the same one in both.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
 We take the types $1$, $3$ and $5$;
 the types $2$, $4$ and $6$ are their mirrors,
 and the argument is the same mutatis mutandis.
 A market buy trades at the best ask in every book and changes nothing else:
 one share in the books $\cks$ and $\hawOne$,
 and $\orderSize_n$ shares, walking the ask, in $\hawQ$.
 Only the best ask can move,
 and removing the lowest asks can only raise it;
 $\pressure_1 = +1$.
 A limit buy of a geometric book is priced below the best ask by \eqref{eq.limitPrice},
 so it rests in full on the bid:
 only the best bid can move,
 and adding size raises it or leaves it.
 In the book $\cks$ a limit buy joins the best bid, or opens a level one tick above it,
 and the same holds;
 or, at a full best bid facing a spread of one tick,
 it trades one share at the best ask,
 and then only the best ask can move, up;
 $\pressure_3 = +1$.
 A withdrawal from the bid changes only the bid,
 and removing size can only lower the best bid or leave it;
 $\pressure_5 = -1$.

 A type moves different best prices in the books $\cks$ and $\hawOne$
 only when $\cks$ sends a limit order to the opposite touch,
 at a spread of one tick.
 If the best prices agree before the event,
 the spread of $\hawOne$ is then one tick as well,
 and \eqref{eq.limitPrice} prices a limit buy of $\hawOne$ at
 $\bestBidPrice_{\arrivalTimes[]_n-} - \tickSizeOfLOB\priceOffset_n$,
 at or behind the best bid,
 so that it moves no best price.
\end{proof}

\begin{corol}\label{corol.partingAtOneQuote}
 The parting time $\partingTime$ is the first time
 at which a best price of the book $\cks$ differs from that of $\hawOne$,
 and at $\partingTime$ exactly one of the two best prices differs.
\end{corol}
\begin{proof}
 Exercise \ref{exercise.partingAtOneQuote}.
\end{proof}

\begin{exercise}\label{exercise.partingAtOneQuote}
 Prove Corollary \ref{corol.partingAtOneQuote}.
 Show from Lemma \ref{lemma.oneBestPrice} that,
 if the best prices of the two books agree before an event,
 at most one of them differs after it,
 and that the mid-prices then differ exactly when one does.
\end{exercise}

The queues of the two books may differ long before $\partingTime$,
and the mid-prices do not see them:
the parting time is decided by the moves of the best prices alone.

\begin{prop}\label{prop.partingProbability}
 Let the best prices of the books $\cks$ and $\hawOne$ agree before the $n$-th event,
 at a spread of $k$ ticks.
 Call the \emph{side of the event} the side on which its message in $\cks$ acts:
 the opposite side for a market order,
 and for a limit order at a full touch facing a spread of one tick;
 its own side otherwise.
 Reading the books before the event,
 let $\size^{\cks}$ and $\size^{\hawOne}$ be the sizes at the touches of that side,
 $\size^{\hawOne}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$ the size resting on it in $\hawOne$,
 and $g$ the number of ticks from the touch of $\hawOne$ to the next occupied price behind it.
 Each book moves the best price of the side of the event,
 by $j^{\cks}$ and $j^{\hawOne}$ ticks,
 and no other best price.
 The event parts the mid-prices exactly when $j^{\cks} \neq j^{\hawOne}$.
 For a limit order the \emph{own touch} is the touch of $\cks$ on the side of the order.
 Given $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$ and the draws of the earlier events,
 $j^{\cks}$ and the probability that the event parts the mid-prices are
 \begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{lll}
   \toprule
   event & $j^{\cks}$ & probability of parting \\
   \midrule
   market order, $\size^{\cks} \geq 2$
   & $0$
   & $\indicator{\size^{\hawOne} = 1}$ \\
   market order, $\size^{\cks} = 1$
   & $1$
   & $1 - \indicator{\size^{\hawOne} = 1, \, g = 1}$ \\
   limit order, $k = 1$, own touch $< \uniformDepth$
   & $0$
   & $0$ \\
   limit order, $k = 1$, own touch $= \uniformDepth$
   & $\indicator{\size^{\cks} = 1}$
   & $\indicator{\size^{\cks} = 1}$ \\
   limit order, $k \geq 2$, $\size^{\cks} < \uniformDepth$
   & $0$
   & $1 - (1-\offsetParameter)^{k-1}$ \\
   limit order, $k \geq 2$, $\size^{\cks} = \uniformDepth$
   & $1$
   & $1 - \offsetParameter(1-\offsetParameter)^{k-2}$ \\
   withdrawal, $\size^{\cks} \geq 2$
   & $0$
   & $\indicator{\size^{\hawOne} = 1} / \size^{\hawOne}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$ \\
   withdrawal, $\size^{\cks} = 1$
   & $1$
   & $1 - \indicator{\size^{\hawOne} = 1, \, g = 1} / \size^{\hawOne}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$ \\
   \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
 \end{center}
\end{prop}
\begin{proof}
 By Lemma \ref{lemma.oneBestPrice} and Corollary \ref{corol.partingAtOneQuote},
 the event parts the mid-prices exactly when the two books move their best prices
 differently.
 By the proof of Lemma \ref{lemma.oneBestPrice},
 each book moves at most the best price of the side of the event:
 the book $\hawOne$ moves no best price when $\cks$ sends a limit order to the opposite
 touch.
 The event therefore parts the mid-prices exactly when $j^{\cks} \neq j^{\hawOne}$.

 \emph{The moves of $\cks$.}
 The opening book has a level on every tick,
 and the placement of Example \ref{example.idealisedPlacement} opens a level only one tick
 inside a touch and empties one only at a touch,
 so each side of $\cks$ holds a level on every tick behind its touch,
 of $\uniformDepth$ shares by part \emph{iii.} of Assumption \ref{assumption.idealisedBook}.
 A best price of $\cks$ therefore moves by one tick or not at all.
 A market order or a withdrawal moves the touch it takes from
 exactly when that touch holds one share.
 A limit order at $k \geq 2$ opens a level one tick inside its touch
 exactly when that touch holds $\uniformDepth$,
 and joins it otherwise.
 At $k = 1$ it joins its touch if that holds less than $\uniformDepth$,
 and otherwise trades one share at the opposite touch,
 the touch of the side of the event,
 which then moves exactly when it holds one share.

 \emph{The moves of $\hawOne$.}
 A market order takes one share from the touch,
 which moves by $g$ ticks exactly when it held one share.
 A limit order at $k \geq 2$ is priced $k - 1 - \priceOffset_n$ ticks inside the touch of
 its side by \eqref{eq.limitPrice},
 so it moves that touch by $j \in \lbrace 1, \dots, k-1 \rbrace$ ticks with probability
 $\offsetParameter(1-\offsetParameter)^{k-1-j}$, by \eqref{eq.priceOffset},
 and leaves it with probability $(1-\offsetParameter)^{k-1}$;
 at $k = 1$ it moves no best price.
 A withdrawal moves the touch exactly when it draws the touch,
 which it does with probability $\size^{\hawOne}/\size^{\hawOne}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$,
 and the touch holds one share;
 it then moves it by $g$ ticks.

 The offsets are independent of $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$ and of the earlier
 draws,
 and the level of a withdrawal is drawn in proportion to the sizes of the book before the
 event,
 which is a function of $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$ and of the earlier draws.
 Each entry of the table is the probability that the two moves differ:
 for a market order,
 $j^{\hawOne} \neq 0$ exactly when $\size^{\hawOne} = 1$,
 and $j^{\hawOne} = 1$ exactly when moreover $g = 1$;
 for a limit order at $k = 1$, $j^{\hawOne} = 0$;
 at $k \geq 2$,
 $j^{\hawOne} \neq 0$ with probability $1 - (1-\offsetParameter)^{k-1}$
 and $j^{\hawOne} \neq 1$ with probability $1 - \offsetParameter(1-\offsetParameter)^{k-2}$;
 for a withdrawal,
 $j^{\hawOne} \neq 0$ with probability
 $\indicator{\size^{\hawOne} = 1} / \size^{\hawOne}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$
 and $j^{\hawOne} = 1$ with probability
 $\indicator{\size^{\hawOne} = 1, \, g = 1} / \size^{\hawOne}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$.
 In both books a market order acts on the side of the event
 as a withdrawal that draws the touch,
 and its rows are those of the withdrawal
 with the probability $\size^{\hawOne}/\size^{\hawOne}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$ of that draw
 set to one.
\end{proof}

\begin{corol}\label{corol.quietBook}
 At a spread of one tick,
 an event moves a best price of the book $\cks$, or of the book $\hawOne$,
 exactly when it takes the last share of one of the touches of that book.
 In particular, while the spread is one tick
 and every touch of the two books holds at least two shares,
 no event moves a best price of either:
 we say that the books are \emph{quiet}.
\end{corol}
\begin{proof}
 We read the proof of Proposition \ref{prop.partingProbability} at $k = 1$.
 In the book $\cks$,
 a market order or a withdrawal takes one share from a touch,
 and a limit order joins its touch or takes one share from the opposite touch;
 a best price moves exactly when the touch that loses the share held one.
 In the book $\hawOne$,
 a limit order rests at or behind the touch of its side and moves no best price,
 and a market order or a withdrawal takes one share from a touch or from behind it;
 a best price moves exactly when that share is the last of a touch.
\end{proof}

\begin{defi}\label{def.firstMoves}
 The \emph{first move} of the book $\cks$ is
 \begin{equation*}
  \firstMove^{\cks} := \inf \big\lbrace t \geq 0 :
  \bestBidPriceOf{\cks}_t \neq \bestBidPrice_0
  \text{ or }
  \bestAskPriceOf{\cks}_t \neq \bestAskPrice_0 \big\rbrace ,
 \end{equation*}
 the first time at which a best price of $\cks$ moves;
 the first move $\firstMove^{\hawOne}$ of the book $\hawOne$ is defined in the same way.
\end{defi}

Before its first move the book $\cks$ is a function of two walks.
Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq.touchWalks}
 \touchWalk{b}(t) := \nthBestBidSize[1]_0 + (\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc(t) ,
 \qquad
 \touchWalk{a}(t) := \nthBestAskSize[1]_0 + (\sideVector{a})^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc(t)
\end{equation}
be the \emph{touch walks},
and let
\begin{equation*}
 \walkMaximum(t) := \max \Big( \uniformDepth ,
 \max_{s \leq t} \max\big( \touchWalk{b}(s), \touchWalk{a}(s) \big) \Big)
\end{equation*}
be their running maximum, floored at $\uniformDepth$.

\begin{prop}\label{prop.cksTouchWalks}
 For $t < \firstMove^{\cks}$,
 \begin{equation*}
  \nthBestBidSize[1,\cks]_t
  = \touchWalk{b}(t) - \big( \walkMaximum(t) - \uniformDepth \big) ,
  \qquad
  \nthBestAskSize[1,\cks]_t
  = \touchWalk{a}(t) - \big( \walkMaximum(t) - \uniformDepth \big) ,
 \end{equation*}
 and
 \begin{equation*}
  \firstMove^{\cks}
  = \inf \Big\lbrace t :
  \walkMaximum(t) - \min\big( \touchWalk{b}(t), \touchWalk{a}(t) \big) = \uniformDepth
  \Big\rbrace .
 \end{equation*}
 In particular $\firstMove^{\cks}$ is $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$-measurable,
 and under balance the means of $\touchWalk{b}(t) - \nthBestBidSize[1]_0$
 and $\touchWalk{a}(t) - \nthBestAskSize[1]_0$ are bounded in $t$.
\end{prop}
\begin{proof}
 Write $x^{b}$ and $x^{a}$ for the two right-hand sides,
 equal to the opening touches at $t = 0$, since $\walkMaximum(0) = \uniformDepth$,
 and both at most $\uniformDepth$, since $\walkMaximum$ dominates both walks.
 We argue by induction on the events before $\firstMove^{\cks}$,
 before which no best price of the book $\cks$ has moved,
 so that its spread is one tick.
 A limit buy raises $\touchWalk{b}$ by one,
 and raises $\walkMaximum$ by one exactly when $\touchWalk{b} = \walkMaximum$ before it,
 that is when $x^{b} = \uniformDepth$.
 If $x^{b} < \uniformDepth$ the order joins the best bid,
 and $x^{b}$ rises by one, as the best bid does.
 If $x^{b} = \uniformDepth$ the best bid is full,
 the order trades one share at the best ask,
 and $x^{b}$ stays while $x^{a}$ falls by one, as the two touches do.
 A market sell and a withdrawal from the bid lower $\touchWalk{b}$ by one,
 leave $\walkMaximum$ unchanged,
 and take one share from the best bid.
 The types of the ask are the same mutatis mutandis.
 By Corollary \ref{corol.quietBook},
 a best price of $\cks$ moves exactly at an event that takes the last share of a touch,
 at which $\min(x^{b}, x^{a})$ reaches zero;
 each event changes $x^{b}$ and $x^{a}$ by at most one,
 which gives the formula for $\firstMove^{\cks}$.
 From no past, \eqref{eq.forwardMeanCountsDeviation} gives
 $\Expectation\,\multiCountingProc(t) - t\,\stationaryIntensity
 = -\excitation\,\meanResponseIntegral(t)\,m$,
 with $m$ the stationary mean of the state,
 which is bounded in $t$ since $\branchingRatio < 1$,
 and $(\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\stationaryIntensity
 = (\sideVector{a})^{\transpose}\stationaryIntensity = 0$ under balance.
\end{proof}

The touch walks count what arrives at each touch.
The cap of part \emph{iii.} of Assumption \ref{assumption.idealisedBook}
removes from both the same quantity $\walkMaximum - \uniformDepth$,
which is the number of limit orders that $\cks$ has sent to the opposite touch,
and so couples the two sides:
$\firstMove^{\cks}$ is the first time one walk falls $\uniformDepth$ below
$\walkMaximum$.

The touches of $\hawOne$ have no cap,
and they depend on the draws of the placement as well as on the path.

\begin{prop}\label{prop.hawkesTouch}
 For $t < \firstMove^{\hawOne}$,
 \begin{equation}\label{eq.hawkesTouch}
  \nthBestBidSize[1,\hawOne]_t
  = \nthBestBidSize[1]_0
  + \sum_{n \, : \, \arrivalTimes[]_n \leq t, \, \event[n] = 3}
  \indicator{\priceOffset_n = 0}
  - \countingProc[2](t)
  - \sum_{n \, : \, \arrivalTimes[]_n \leq t, \, \event[n] = 5}
  \indicator{p_n = \bestBidPrice_{\arrivalTimes[]_n-}} ,
 \end{equation}
 and likewise for the best ask, with the types $4$, $1$ and $6$.
 Given $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$ and the draws of the earlier events,
 the event $\lbrace \priceOffset_n = 0 \rbrace$ has probability $\offsetParameter$,
 and the event $\lbrace p_n = \bestBidPrice_{\arrivalTimes[]_n-} \rbrace$ has probability
 $\nthBestBidSize[1,\hawOne]_{\arrivalTimes[]_n-}
 / \size^{b,\hawOne}_{\arrivalTimes[]_n-}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$,
 the denominator being \eqref{eq.sideSize}.
 The first move $\firstMove^{\hawOne}$ is the first time
 at which one of the two right-hand sides reaches zero.
\end{prop}
\begin{proof}
 Before $\firstMove^{\hawOne}$ no best price of $\hawOne$ has moved,
 so that its spread is the opening tick.
 A limit buy is priced at
 $\bestAskPrice_{\arrivalTimes[]_n-} - \tickSizeOfLOB(1 + \priceOffset_n)
 = \bestBidPrice_{\arrivalTimes[]_n-} - \tickSizeOfLOB\priceOffset_n$
 by \eqref{eq.limitPrice},
 so it joins the best bid exactly when $\priceOffset_n = 0$,
 which has probability $\offsetParameter$ by \eqref{eq.priceOffset},
 and goes behind it otherwise.
 A market sell takes one share from the best bid.
 A withdrawal from the bid takes one share from the level it draws,
 which is the best bid with the probability stated,
 by Definition \ref{def.geometricPlacement}~\emph{iii.}
 The other three types act on the ask.
 By Corollary \ref{corol.quietBook},
 a best price of $\hawOne$ moves exactly at an event that takes the last share of a touch,
 at which one of the two right-hand sides reaches zero;
 each event changes them by at most one.
\end{proof}

Before their first moves the touches of the two books are walks
from openings of the order of $\uniformDepth$,
and they differ in their drift.
By \eqref{eq.hawkesTouch}
the best bid of $\hawOne$ gains a share at a limit buy with probability $\offsetParameter$,
and loses one at every market sell
and at a withdrawal from the bid that draws it.
Its drift,
$\offsetParameter\stationaryIntensity[3] - \stationaryIntensity[2]
- \stationaryIntensity[5] \, \nthBestBidSize[1,\hawOne]_t / \size^{b,\hawOne}_t(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$,
is of order one per unit of time,
and if $\offsetParameter\stationaryIntensity[3] < \stationaryIntensity[2]$ it is negative,
so that the touch empties in a time of the order of $\uniformDepth$.
The touch walks of $\cks$ receive every event of their side,
and under balance they have no drift:
the drawdown that gives $\firstMove^{\cks}$ in Proposition \ref{prop.cksTouchWalks},
of the order of $\uniformDepth$,
comes of their fluctuations alone,
in a time of the order of $\uniformDepth^{2}$.
At a fixed $\uniformDepth$ either book may move first.
As $\uniformDepth$ grows the two scales separate,
and we let $\uniformDepth \to \infty$,
with opening touches that grow in proportion:
the counts follow their means over horizons proportional to $\uniformDepth$,
and each touch of $\hawOne$ follows an ordinary differential equation.

\begin{prop}\label{prop.fluidTouch}
 Let $\uniformDepth \to \infty$, with $L$, $\offsetParameter$ and the specification fixed,
 and with $\size^{x,1}_0 / \uniformDepth \to \fluidTouch^{x}(0) \in (0, 1]$
 for each side $x \in \lbrace b, a \rbrace$.
 Let $L_x := L - 1 + \fluidTouch^{x}(0)$,
 the limit of $\size^{x}_0(\tickSizeOfLOB\N) / \uniformDepth$,
 and let $\fluidTouch^{x}$ solve
 \begin{equation}\label{eq.fluidTouch}
  (\fluidTouch^{x})\derivative(s)
  = \offsetParameter\stationaryIntensity[3] - \stationaryIntensity[2]
  - \frac{\stationaryIntensity[5]}{L_x} \, \fluidTouch^{x}(s)
 \end{equation}
 from $\fluidTouch^{x}(0)$,
 that is
 $\fluidTouch^{x}(s) = \fluidTouch^{x}_{\infty}
 + (\fluidTouch^{x}(0) - \fluidTouch^{x}_{\infty}) \, e^{-\stationaryIntensity[5] s / L_x}$
 with
 $\fluidTouch^{x}_{\infty} := L_x (\offsetParameter\stationaryIntensity[3]
 - \stationaryIntensity[2]) / \stationaryIntensity[5]$.
 Let $s_1 > 0$ be such that $\fluidTouch^{b}$ and $\fluidTouch^{a}$ are positive on
 $[0, s_1]$.
 Then, in probability,
 \begin{equation*}
  \sup_{s \leq s_1} \Big\vert
  \frac{\nthBestBidSize[1,\hawOne]_{\uniformDepth s}}{\uniformDepth} - \fluidTouch^{b}(s)
  \Big\vert \to 0 ,
  \qquad
  \sup_{s \leq s_1} \Big\vert
  \frac{\nthBestAskSize[1,\hawOne]_{\uniformDepth s}}{\uniformDepth} - \fluidTouch^{a}(s)
  \Big\vert \to 0 ,
 \end{equation*}
 and $\Prob\big( \firstMove^{\hawOne} > \uniformDepth s_1 \big) \to 1$.
\end{prop}
\begin{proof}
 We proceed in three steps.

 \emph{A law of large numbers for the counts.}
 Let $\martingale := \multiCountingProc - \compensator$.
 From no past $\decayedCounts(0+) = 0$,
 and integrating \eqref{eq.stateDynamics} and \eqref{eq.intensityFromState} over $(0, t]$,
 as in the derivation of \eqref{eq.countIncrementIdentity},
 gives at every $t$
 \begin{equation}\label{eq.countsFromNoPast}
  \multiCountingProc(t) - t\,\stationaryIntensity
  = (I - \branchingMatrix)^{-1} \martingale(t)
  - \branchingMatrix (I - \branchingMatrix)^{-1} \decayedCounts(t+) .
 \end{equation}
 By Doob's inequality\footnote{%
  Doob's inequality bounds a square-integrable martingale $X$ by
  $\Expectation \sup_{t \leq T} X(t)^{2} \leq 4\,\Expectation\,X(T)^{2}$.
  Gronwall's inequality, in the form used below,
  states that a bounded function $f \geq 0$ with
  $f(s) \leq a + b \int_{(0,s]} f(s'-) \, \mu(ds')$,
  for constants $a, b \geq 0$ and a measure $\mu$ with finitely many atoms on $(0,s]$,
  satisfies $f(s) \leq a \, e^{b \mu((0,s])}$.}
 $\Expectation \sup_{t \leq T} \martingale_e(t)^{2}
 \leq 4\,\Expectation\,\martingale_e(T)^{2} = 4\,\Expectation\,\countingProc[e](T) = O(T)$,
 by \eqref{eq.forwardMeanCountsDeviation}.
 The proof of Theorem \ref{thm.lyapunov} starts from $\decayedCounts(0) = 0$
 and shows that the second moment of the state converges,
 so the second moments of $\decayedCounts(t+)$ are bounded in $t$,
 and so are those of
 $\countingProc[e](k+1) - \countingProc[e](k)
 = \decayedCounts_e((k+1)+) - \decayedCounts_e(k+)
 + \decay \int_{k}^{k+1} \decayedCounts_e(t) \, dt$.
 Since
 $\sup_{t \in (k, k+1]} \decayedCounts_e(t+)
 \leq \decayedCounts_e(k+) + \countingProc[e](k+1) - \countingProc[e](k)$,
 Chebyshev's inequality and a union bound over the unit intervals of $[0, T]$ give
 $\Prob\big( \sup_{t \leq T} \vert \decayedCounts(t+) \vert > T^{3/4} \big)
 = O(T^{-1/2})$.
 Taking $T = \uniformDepth s_1$ in \eqref{eq.countsFromNoPast},
 and bounding its martingale term by Doob's and Chebyshev's inequalities,
 we find, with
 $\multiCountingProc^{\uniformDepth}(s) := \multiCountingProc(\uniformDepth s)/\uniformDepth$,
 \begin{equation*}
  \sup_{s \leq s_1} \big\vert
  \multiCountingProc^{\uniformDepth}(s) - s\,\stationaryIntensity
  \big\vert \to 0
 \end{equation*}
 in probability,
 and in particular, by \eqref{eq.sideSize} and balance,
 $\size^{b,\hawOne}_{\uniformDepth s}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N) / \uniformDepth \to L_b$
 uniformly on $[0, s_1]$.

 \emph{The draws.}
 For a limit buy let $\delta_n := \indicator{\priceOffset_n = 0} - \offsetParameter$,
 for a withdrawal from the bid
 $\delta_n := \indicator{p_n = \bestBidPrice_{\arrivalTimes[]_n-}}
 - \nthBestBidSize[1,\hawOne]_{\arrivalTimes[]_n-}
 / \size^{b,\hawOne}_{\arrivalTimes[]_n-}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N)$,
 and $\delta_n := 0$ for the other types.
 Given $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$ and the draws of the events before the $n$-th,
 $\delta_n$ has mean zero, by Definition \ref{def.geometricPlacement},
 and $\vert \delta_n \vert \leq 1$.
 Given $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$,
 the partial sums $J_3(t)$ of the $\delta_n$ over the limit buys of $(0, t]$,
 and $J_5(t)$ over the withdrawals from the bid,
 therefore form martingales in the order of the events,
 with orthogonal increments,
 and Doob's inequality gives
 $\Expectation \sup_{t \leq T} J_e(t)^{2} \leq 4\,\Expectation\,\countingProc[e](T) = O(T)$,
 so that $\sup_{s \leq s_1} \vert J_e(\uniformDepth s) \vert / \uniformDepth \to 0$ in
 probability, for $e = 3, 5$.

 \emph{Gronwall's inequality.}
 Write
 $x(s) := \nthBestBidSize[1,\hawOne]_{\uniformDepth s} / \uniformDepth$
 and
 $y(s) := \size^{b,\hawOne}_{\uniformDepth s}(\tickSizeOfLOB\N) / \uniformDepth$,
 and let $\tilde{x}(s)$ be the right-hand side of \eqref{eq.hawkesTouch} at
 $t = \uniformDepth s$, divided by $\uniformDepth$,
 for $s \leq \firstMove^{\hawOne}/\uniformDepth$.
 Then $\tilde{x} = x$ on $[0, \firstMove^{\hawOne}/\uniformDepth)$,
 and $\tilde{x}(\firstMove^{\hawOne}/\uniformDepth) = 0$
 if the event at $\firstMove^{\hawOne}$ takes the last share of the best bid,
 by Corollary \ref{corol.quietBook}.
 By the definition of $J_3$ and $J_5$,
 \begin{equation*}
  \tilde{x}(s)
  = \frac{\nthBestBidSize[1]_0}{\uniformDepth}
  + \offsetParameter\, \countingProc[3]^{\uniformDepth}(s)
  - \countingProc[2]^{\uniformDepth}(s)
  - \int_{(0,s]} \frac{\tilde{x}(s'-)}{y(s'-)} \, d\countingProc[5]^{\uniformDepth}(s')
  + \frac{J_{3}(\uniformDepth s) - J_{5}(\uniformDepth s)}{\uniformDepth} ,
 \end{equation*}
 while
 $\fluidTouch^{b}(s) = \fluidTouch^{b}(0)
 + (\offsetParameter\stationaryIntensity[3] - \stationaryIntensity[2]) s
 - \int_{0}^{s} \stationaryIntensity[5] \fluidTouch^{b}(s')/L_b \, ds'$.
 Subtract,
 and use
 $\vert \tilde{x}/y - \fluidTouch^{b}/L_b \vert
 \leq \vert \tilde{x} - \fluidTouch^{b} \vert / y + \vert \fluidTouch^{b} \vert \,
 \vert 1/y - 1/L_b \vert$
 with $y \geq L_b/2$,
 which holds on $[0, s_1]$ with probability tending to one by step 1.
 For $s \leq s_1 \wedge \firstMove^{\hawOne}/\uniformDepth$ this gives
 \begin{equation*}
  \vert \tilde{x}(s) - \fluidTouch^{b}(s) \vert
  \leq \bar{\delta}_{\uniformDepth}
  + \frac{2}{L_b} \int_{(0,s]} \vert \tilde{x}(s'-) - \fluidTouch^{b}(s') \vert \,
  d\countingProc[5]^{\uniformDepth}(s') ,
 \end{equation*}
 where $\bar{\delta}_{\uniformDepth}$ is the supremum over $s \leq s_1$ of
 $\vert \nthBestBidSize[1]_0 / \uniformDepth - \fluidTouch^{b}(0) \vert
 + \offsetParameter \vert \countingProc[3]^{\uniformDepth}(s) - \stationaryIntensity[3] s \vert
 + \vert \countingProc[2]^{\uniformDepth}(s) - \stationaryIntensity[2] s \vert
 + \vert J_3(\uniformDepth s) - J_5(\uniformDepth s) \vert / \uniformDepth
 + \sup_{[0,s_1]} \vert \fluidTouch^{b} \vert \, \sup_{[0,s_1]} \vert 1/y - 1/L_b \vert \,
 \countingProc[5]^{\uniformDepth}(s_1)
 + \vert \int_{0}^{s} \fluidTouch^{b}(s') \,
 ( d\countingProc[5]^{\uniformDepth}(s') - \stationaryIntensity[5] \, ds' ) \vert / L_b$.
 The first five terms tend to zero in probability,
 by the hypothesis on the opening and by steps 1 and 2;
 so does the last,
 which an integration by parts writes as
 $\vert \fluidTouch^{b}(s) ( \countingProc[5]^{\uniformDepth}(s) - \stationaryIntensity[5] s )
 - \int_{0}^{s} (\fluidTouch^{b})\derivative(s')
 ( \countingProc[5]^{\uniformDepth}(s') - \stationaryIntensity[5] s' ) \, ds' \vert / L_b$.
 Gronwall's inequality for the measure $d\countingProc[5]^{\uniformDepth}$,
 whose atoms are $1/\uniformDepth$,
 gives
 \begin{equation*}
  \sup_{s \leq s_1 \wedge \firstMove^{\hawOne}/\uniformDepth}
  \vert \tilde{x}(s) - \fluidTouch^{b}(s) \vert
  \leq \bar{\delta}_{\uniformDepth} \, e^{2 \countingProc[5]^{\uniformDepth}(s_1)/L_b} \to 0
 \end{equation*}
 in probability.
 On the event that $\firstMove^{\hawOne} \leq \uniformDepth s_1$
 and that the event at $\firstMove^{\hawOne}$ empties the best bid,
 the left-hand side is at least
 $\fluidTouch^{b}(\firstMove^{\hawOne}/\uniformDepth) \geq \min_{[0,s_1]} \fluidTouch^{b} > 0$,
 since $\tilde{x}$ vanishes at $\firstMove^{\hawOne}/\uniformDepth$,
 so that this event has probability tending to zero.
 The ask is the same, from its own opening,
 with the types $4$, $1$ and $6$,
 whose stationary intensities are those of $3$, $2$ and $5$.
 Hence $\Prob(\firstMove^{\hawOne} > \uniformDepth s_1) \to 1$,
 and on that event $x = \tilde{x}$ on $[0, s_1]$,
 which gives the two limits.
\end{proof}

\begin{exercise}\label{exercise.fluidTouch}
 Solve \eqref{eq.fluidTouch}.
 Show that $\fluidTouch^{x} > 0$ on $[0, \infty)$ if and only if
 $\offsetParameter\stationaryIntensity[3] \geq \stationaryIntensity[2]$,
 and that otherwise $\fluidTouch^{x}$ reaches zero at the time $\fluidEmptying_{x}$ of
 \eqref{eq.fluidEmptying}.
\end{exercise}

\begin{corol}\label{corol.twoRegimes}
 Let $\uniformDepth \to \infty$ as in Proposition \ref{prop.fluidTouch}.
 \begin{enumerate}[label={\emph{\roman{*}}.}]
  \item If $\offsetParameter\stationaryIntensity[3] < \stationaryIntensity[2]$,
        the fluid touch of the side $x$ reaches zero at
        \begin{equation}\label{eq.fluidEmptying}
         \fluidEmptying_{x}
         = \frac{L_x}{\stationaryIntensity[5]}
         \log \Big( 1 + \frac{\stationaryIntensity[5] \, \fluidTouch^{x}(0)}
         {L_x (\stationaryIntensity[2] - \offsetParameter\stationaryIntensity[3])} \Big) ,
        \end{equation}
        and, with $\fluidEmptying := \fluidEmptying_{b} \wedge \fluidEmptying_{a}$,
        $\partingTime/\uniformDepth \to \fluidEmptying$
        and $\firstMove^{\hawOne}/\uniformDepth \to \fluidEmptying$ in probability,
        with $\Prob(\partingTime = \firstMove^{\hawOne}) \to 1$:
        a touch of $\hawOne$ empties first,
        and the first move is that of $\hawOne$.
  \item If $\offsetParameter\stationaryIntensity[3] \geq \stationaryIntensity[2]$,
        the fluid touches are positive on $[0, \infty)$,
        and $\partingTime/\uniformDepth \to \infty$ in probability.
 \end{enumerate}
\end{corol}
\begin{proof}
 By step 1 of the proof of Proposition \ref{prop.fluidTouch} and balance,
 $\sup_{t \leq \uniformDepth s_1}
 \vert \touchWalk{b}(t) - \nthBestBidSize[1]_0 \vert / \uniformDepth$
 and its ask counterpart tend to zero in probability for every $s_1$.
 Since $\walkMaximum(0) = \uniformDepth$
 and the opening touches are at least
 $\uniformDepth \min(\fluidTouch^{b}(0), \fluidTouch^{a}(0))/2$
 for $\uniformDepth$ large,
 with probability tending to one
 $\walkMaximum - \min(\touchWalk{b}, \touchWalk{a}) < \uniformDepth$
 on $[0, \uniformDepth s_1]$.
 By Proposition \ref{prop.cksTouchWalks}, then,
 $\Prob(\firstMove^{\cks} > \uniformDepth s_1) \to 1$,
 and the touches of $\cks$ hold at least two shares on $[0, \uniformDepth s_1]$
 with probability tending to one.

 \emph{i.}
 The formula for $\fluidEmptying$ is Exercise \ref{exercise.fluidTouch}.
 Let $s_{-} < \fluidEmptying < s_{+}$,
 and let $x$ be a side with $\fluidEmptying_{x} = \fluidEmptying$.
 Proposition \ref{prop.fluidTouch} on $[0, s_{-}]$ gives
 $\Prob\big( \firstMove^{\hawOne} > \uniformDepth s_{-} \big) \to 1$.
 Its proof, up to its last display,
 does not use the sign of $\fluidTouch^{x}$;
 run on $[0, s_{+}]$, where $\fluidTouch^{x}$ continues the solution,
 it shows that on
 $\lbrace \firstMove^{\hawOne} > \uniformDepth s_{+} \rbrace$
 the touch of the side $x$ at $\uniformDepth s_{+}$ is close to
 $\uniformDepth\fluidTouch^{x}(s_{+}) < 0$,
 which a size cannot be,
 so that
 $\Prob\big( \firstMove^{\hawOne} \leq \uniformDepth s_{+} \big) \to 1$.
 With probability tending to one, therefore,
 $\firstMove^{\hawOne} < \firstMove^{\cks}$,
 and at $\firstMove^{\hawOne}$ the touches of $\cks$ hold at least two shares,
 so that, by Corollary \ref{corol.quietBook},
 the event at $\firstMove^{\hawOne}$ moves a best price of $\hawOne$ and none of $\cks$,
 and $\partingTime = \firstMove^{\hawOne}$.

 \emph{ii.}
 Here $\fluidTouch^{b}$ and $\fluidTouch^{a}$ are positive on every $[0, s_1]$,
 and Proposition \ref{prop.fluidTouch} gives
 $\Prob(\firstMove^{\hawOne} > \uniformDepth s_1) \to 1$ for every $s_1$.
 Now $\partingTime \geq \firstMove^{\cks} \wedge \firstMove^{\hawOne}$,
 and both terms exceed $\uniformDepth s_1$ with probability tending to one.
\end{proof}

The regime is decided by the sign of
$\offsetParameter\stationaryIntensity[3] - \stationaryIntensity[2]$:
the rate at which limit buys join the best bid of $\hawOne$
against the rate at which market sells take from it.
The withdrawals take from the touch in proportion to its share of the side,
and pull it towards $\fluidTouch^{x}_{\infty}\uniformDepth$.
In the first regime the limit decides which book moves first,
and gives $\partingTime$ to first order, as $\uniformDepth\fluidEmptying$;
the book $\cks$ plays no part in it.
At a finite $\uniformDepth$
the touch of $\hawOne$ fluctuates about $\uniformDepth\fluidTouch^{x}(t/\uniformDepth)$
by an amount of the order of $\sqrt{\uniformDepth}$,
so that $\partingTime/\uniformDepth$ falls short of $\fluidEmptying$ on average,
and by more near the threshold,
where the fluid touch meets zero at a small slope.
In the second regime the corollary says only that neither book moves on the scale
$\uniformDepth$,
and what can be said beyond it is heuristic.

The book $\cks$ does not depend on $\offsetParameter$,
and it moves by the fluctuations of its touch walks.
By the functional central limit theorem of \cite{BDHM13som},
applied to $(\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc$
and $(\sideVector{a})^{\transpose}\multiCountingProc$,
whose drifts vanish under balance,
the walks move by $\uniformDepth$ in a time of the order of
$\uniformDepth^{2} / (\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\countCovarianceRate\,\sideVector{b}$.
On that scale $\firstMove^{\cks}$ is the drawdown of Proposition \ref{prop.cksTouchWalks}
read on a pair of correlated Brownian motions in place of the walks,
and it remains a function of the path.

The touch of $\hawOne$ relaxes,
at the rate $\stationaryIntensity[5]/(L_x\uniformDepth)$ per share of deviation,
towards $\fluidTouch^{x}_{\infty}/L_x$ times the size of its side,
which by \eqref{eq.sideSize} is $L_x\uniformDepth$ plus a walk without drift,
and it fluctuates about that centre by an amount of the order of $\sqrt{\uniformDepth}$.
It reaches zero by a large deviation of that fluctuation,
or when its side is nearly exhausted,
which takes a time of the order of
$(L\uniformDepth)^{2} / (\sideVector{b})^{\transpose}\countCovarianceRate\,\sideVector{b}$,
$L^{2}$ times the scale of $\cks$.
Both first moves are then on the scale $\uniformDepth^{2}$,
and which comes first is decided by $L$ and not by $\uniformDepth$:
on a book of many levels $\cks$ moves first,
and $\partingTime = \firstMove^{\cks}$ is a function of the path.

\subsubsection{The discrepancy of the mid-prices}
\label{sec.discrepancy}

After the parting time the two mid-prices move apart.
We study their difference:
its mean, which is zero when the two opening touches are equal,
and its variance,
which we split into the part that the path of events decides
and the part that the placement of the orders adds.

\begin{defi}\label{def.discrepancy}
 The \emph{discrepancy} of the books $\cks$ and $\hawOne$ is
 \begin{equation*}
  \discrepancy_t := \midPriceOf{\cks}_t - \midPriceOf{\hawOne}_t .
 \end{equation*}
\end{defi}

Both mid-prices lie on the grid $\tfrac{\tickSizeOfLOB}{2}\Z$ of half ticks,
and so does $\discrepancy_t$.

\begin{prop}\label{prop.discrepancyMoments}
 For every $t$ on the horizon considered:
 \begin{enumerate}[label={\emph{\roman{*}}.}]
  \item $\discrepancy_t = 0$ on $\lbrace \partingTime > t \rbrace$;
  \item if $\nthBestBidSize[1]_0 = \nthBestAskSize[1]_0$,
        the law of $\discrepancy_t$ is symmetric about zero,
        and in particular $\Expectation\,\discrepancy_t = 0$;
  \item under the hypothesis of \emph{ii.},
        $\Variance\,\discrepancy_t
        = \Expectation\big[ \discrepancy_t^{2} ; \, \partingTime \leq t \big]
        \geq (\tickSizeOfLOB/2)^{2} \, \Prob(\discrepancy_t \neq 0)$;
  \item $\Variance\,\discrepancy_t
        = \Variance\big( \Expectation[ \discrepancy_t \mid \sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc} ]
        \big)
        + \Expectation\big[ \Variance( \midPriceOf{\hawOne}_t \mid
        \sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc} ) \big]$.%
        \footnote{%
         Statement \emph{iv.} is the law of total variance given the path,
         and it needs a conditioning event
         that is $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$-measurable.
         With orders of one share,
         the part of $G$ that excludes an empty side is:
         the sides of $\hawOne$ are \eqref{eq.sideSize},
         and the book $\cks$ is a function of the path.
         The part that excludes the floor depends on the offsets, and is not;
         statement \emph{iv.} holds exactly with $G$ replaced by its first part.
         With random sizes the emptying of a side of $\hawQ$ depends on the sizes,
         and the split for $\hawQ$ is a statement on the draws that keep its sides
         occupied.}
 \end{enumerate}
 Write $\partingTime^{\hawQ}$ and $\discrepancy^{\hawQ}_t$ for the parting time and the
 discrepancy of the books $\cks$ and $\hawQ$.
 The four statements hold for them, with $\hawQ$ in place of $\hawOne$.
\end{prop}

We call the two terms of \emph{iv.} the \emph{flow part} and the \emph{placement part}
of the variance:
the variance of what the path alone decides,
and the variance that the draws of $\hawOne$ add given the path.

\begin{proof}
 \emph{i.} is the definition of $\partingTime$.

 \emph{ii.}
 The reflection $p \mapsto 2\midPrice_0 - p$ maps the tick grid to itself,
 since $2\midPrice_0 = \bestBidPrice_0 + \bestAskPrice_0$,
 exchanges the bid and the ask,
 and maps the opening book of Definition \ref{def.threeBooks} to itself,
 its two touches being equal.
 On $G$,
 every message of the path and of its mirror is that of
 Example \ref{example.idealisedPlacement}
 or of parts \emph{i.} to \emph{iii.} of Definition \ref{def.geometricPlacement},
 neither the floor nor the price of an empty side being used,
 and these commute with the reflection, the type being replaced by its mirror:
 the rules of Example \ref{example.idealisedPlacement} are the same on the two sides;
 a limit buy at $\bestAskPrice - \tickSizeOfLOB(1+\priceOffset_n)$
 reflects to a limit sell at the reflected best bid plus $\tickSizeOfLOB(1+\priceOffset_n)$;
 and a withdrawal that takes the first level of its side,
 counted from the touch,
 at which the cumulated size exceeds $U_n$ times the size of the side,
 reflects to the withdrawal of the other side that the same $U_n$ defines,
 since the reflection maps the levels of one side, counted from its touch,
 to those of the other, counted from its touch.
 By induction on the events,
 on $G$ the books folded from the mirror are the reflections of those folded from the path,
 so that the discrepancy of the mirror is $-\discrepancy_t$.
 By \eqref{eq.directionSymmetry} the mirror has intensity
 $\swapMatrix\intensity[ ] = \baseIntensity + \excitation\swapMatrix\decayedCounts$,
 so it is a Hawkes process with the specification of the path, with no past,
 and it has the law of the path;
 the draws $(\priceOffset_n, U_n, \orderSize_n)$ are independent of both.
 The exchange of the path with its mirror therefore preserves the law of the path and of
 its draws,
 and it leaves $G$ invariant,
 so that on $G$ the law of $-\discrepancy_t$ is that of $\discrepancy_t$.

 \emph{iii.}
 By \emph{i.} and \emph{ii.},
 $\Variance\,\discrepancy_t = \Expectation\,\discrepancy_t^{2}
 = \Expectation[\discrepancy_t^{2} ; \, \partingTime \leq t]$,
 and $\discrepancy_t \neq 0$ implies $\vert \discrepancy_t \vert \geq \tickSizeOfLOB/2$.

 \emph{iv.} is the law of total variance:
 the book $\cks$ is $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$-measurable,
 so that
 $\Variance(\discrepancy_t \mid \sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc})
 = \Variance(\midPriceOf{\hawOne}_t \mid \sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc})$.

 None of the four arguments uses the size of the orders,
 and the reflection leaves the sizes unchanged,
 so they hold for $\hawQ$.
\end{proof}

The order flow imbalance of any book is tied to the moves of its best prices.
At an event that leaves the best bid in place,
the bid terms of \eqref{eq.orderFlowContribution} are the change in the size at the best bid.
At an event that moves it,
they differ from that change by the size at the lower of the two best bids,
signed by the move,
and the ask is the same with the higher of the two best asks.
Over a window, therefore,
the imbalance less the change in the size at the best bid,
plus the change at the best ask,
is the sum, over the moves of the two best prices,
of the size at the outer of the two values of each,
signed by the direction of the move.
Under Assumption \ref{assumption.idealisedBook}
every move is by one tick and that size is $\uniformDepth$,
and the identity is \eqref{eq.idealisedUpdate}.
The book $\hawOne$ departs from it in three ways:
the events it places behind its touches contribute nothing to its imbalance,
its touches at $t$ differ from those of $\cks$,
and it moves through queues that need not hold $\uniformDepth$.
Then $2\uniformDepth\discrepancy_t/\tickSizeOfLOB$ is the sum of three terms, one for each.
Given the path,
an event is placed behind its touch with a probability read off the book before it:
$(1-\offsetParameter)^{k}$ for a limit order at a spread of $k$ ticks,
one less the share of the touch in its side for a withdrawal,
and zero for a market order.
The first term is the sum of these probabilities, signed by $\pressure$,
plus a martingale whose variance given the path is known.

What can be said of the variance depends on the regime.
In the second,
on horizons short against the life of a side,
the best prices of $\hawOne$ seldom move,
and $\discrepancy_t$ is close to $\midPriceOf{\cks}_t - \midPrice_0$,
a function of the path.
By \eqref{eq.idealisedUpdate} and \eqref{eq.flowVolatility}
the variance of $\discrepancy_t/\tickSizeOfLOB$ then grows at about
$\pressure^{\transpose}\countCovarianceRate\,\pressure/(2\uniformDepth)^{2}$
per unit of time,
nearly all of it flow part.
In the first regime both mid-prices move.
If each type of event were placed behind its touch with a fixed probability,
and $\hawOne$ moved as a book of a fixed depth per tick,
the variance would grow linearly in $t$,
its flow part coming from the difference of the impacts of one event on the two mid-prices,
and its placement part from the draws.
Neither holds on the books of $\hawOne$,
and the split they suggest is a first guess.

Random sizes change the comparison in kind.
A market order or a withdrawal moves a touch of $\hawQ$
whenever its size reaches the size resting there,
possibly across several levels,
and the lognormal law has unbounded support,
so the books $\cks$ and $\hawQ$ are never quiet.
Counted in units of $\meanSize$ the sizes have mean one,
and to first order they leave the fluid touch and the two regimes as they are.
The mean of $\discrepancy^{\hawQ}_t$ is still zero, by Proposition \ref{prop.discrepancyMoments},
and its variance gains what the count of events does not see:
the size of each order enters the placement part,
and it makes random, given the path,
both the depth through which $\hawQ$ moves and its whole-book imbalance.
The size on a side of $\hawQ$ is a walk of a larger variance than \eqref{eq.sideSize},
and its sides empty sooner.

How fast the variance of the discrepancy grows in each regime,
how much of it the path decides,
and how much the sizes add to it,
are left to measurement.
