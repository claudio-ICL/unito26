\chapter{Market microstructure}
\label{chap.microstructure}

This chapter describes how prices are formed in order-driven markets.
It begins with the mechanism --- what an order is, how the exchange matches orders,
and what the limit order book is at any instant ---
and with the reconstruction of that book from the message file of a recorded session.
It then develops the theory of point processes and of self-excitation,
which is the apparatus we use to model the arrival of orders
and to read the statistical properties of a trading epoch.
Section \ref{sec.priceFormation} is the one the others are for:
price formation.
It asks how a price emerges from the flow of trading orders,
and which statistics of that flow forecast the movements of the mid-price.
Section \ref{sec.lobsterEmpirics} is the assignment of the chapter,
and puts those statements to recorded data.

\cite{CKS14pri} introduces the order flow imbalance
and is the reference of Section \ref{sec.priceFormation};
it is the reading this chapter expects.
\citet[Chapters 1--3]{FPR13mar} sets out the institutions of an order-driven market
and the economics of liquidity,
and \cite{Bou18tra} treats the statistics of the limit order book at length;
either serves the student who wants to read further.
Section \ref{sec.readingList} collects the reading of the whole course.
