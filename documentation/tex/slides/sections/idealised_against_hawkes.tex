When the two books part

How far apart their mid-prices go
\note{
The identity of the idealised book is exact under an assumption that the book generated by the
flow never satisfies. This class measures what the assumption costs. We place the events of one
Hawkes process in two ways: so that the assumption holds, and by the geometric rule of the
simulator. Both books are folded from the same opening book by the same events, and we compare
them, first through the time at which they part, then through the distance between their
mid-prices once they have parted.

\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{From a Hawkes event to a unit order}
{\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}
 \REQUIRE the book before $\arrivalTimes[]_n$, and the type $\event[n]$, of direction $d$
 \IF{$\event[n]$ would take the last share of a side}
 \STATE stop: $\arrivalTimes[]_n$ is the breach
 \ELSIF{$\event[n]$ is a market order}
 \STATE submit one share at the best quote of the side $-d$
 \ELSIF{$\event[n]$ is a withdrawal}
 \STATE cancel one share at the best quote of the side $d$
 \ELSIF{the best quote of the side $d$ holds less than $\uniformDepth$}
 \STATE submit one share there
 \ELSIF{one tick beyond it is the best quote of the side $-d$}
 \STATE submit one share at that quote, where it trades
 \ELSE
 \STATE submit one share one tick beyond it, a new best quote
 \ENDIF
\end{algorithmic}
}

\codehl{IdealisedFlow}: nothing drawn beyond the event

\codehl{AssumptionBreach}: the touch holds one share, the tick behind none
\note{
This is the placement of the package, in the module of the idealised book. The function that
builds an opening book takes the two touches and the number of levels, and puts
$\uniformDepth$ on every tick behind each touch. The stream checks the book against the
assumption once, before the first message, since the flow keeps the assumption true but cannot
make it so. Then each event of the Hawkes process becomes one message, read against the book as
it stands, and the matching is left to the ordinary book.
A market order is submitted as a limit order of one share at the opposite touch, so its market
part is its whole size. The sizes are units because part ii of the assumption needs them: an
order of two shares can overshoot the cap, or reach past a touch that holds one.
The behind-the-touch queues are contiguous, so a side is about to empty exactly when its touch
holds one share and the tick behind it holds none. That event is not moved or dropped: it ends
the stream.
}
\end{frame}

Geometric placement: the assumption at no $\offsetParameter$

The sell types are the mirror image. The first column is the placement of the last frame, under

The second column is the placement of the simulator. It satisfies the assumption at no value of

\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{One path of events, two placements}
\begin{assumption}
\label{assumption.twoPlacements}
 From one opening book satisfying part iii, the \emph{idealised book} folds the idealised
 placement of $(\arrivalTimes[]_n, \event[n])$, the \emph{geometric book} the geometric
 placement of $(\arrivalTimes[]_n, \event[n], \orderSize_n)$.
\end{assumption}

Superscripts $\idealisedBook$, $\geometricBook$: the book a quantity is read on

$\breachTime$: the first arrival the idealised placement cannot place

Idealised book: $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$-measurable, defined on $[0, \breachTime)$
\note{
Both sides of the opening book are occupied. $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$ is the
$\sigma$-algebra of the arrival times and the types. The idealised placement draws nothing
beyond the events, so the idealised book is a function of the opening book and of the path;
the geometric book also reads the sizes and the offsets.
We take the best bid of either book above one tick: the geometric placement floors a limit
price at one tick, and the statements that read a limit price assume no floor is met.
Now to notebook 10, section 1. To compare two placements the events must be the same and only
the placement may differ. A simulator and a Hawkes process on the same seed give different
paths, since every mark drawn shifts the draws that follow, so the events are drawn once and
each placement reads its own replay of them.
}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{The target offset}
\begin{defi}
\label{def.targetOffset}
 For a limit event at $\arrivalTimes[]_n < \breachTime$,
 $\targetOffset_n := \LOBspread^{\idealisedBook}_{\arrivalTimes[]_n-}/\tickSizeOfLOB - 1
 - \indicator{\size^{\idealisedBook}_{\arrivalTimes[]_n-} = \uniformDepth}$.
\end{defi}

$\size^{\idealisedBook}_{\arrivalTimes[]_n-}$: the size at the best quote of its own side

Until the distance: $\orderSize_n = 1$, $\baseIntensity_5 = \baseIntensity_6 = 0$
\note{
In the first half of the class the orders have size one and the flow has no withdrawals: the
baselines of the two withdrawal types are zero and their rows and columns of $\excitation$ are
null, so these types carry no event almost surely, and the path is a Hawkes process on the four
types of market and limit orders.
The target offset is the offset at which the geometric rule prices the order where the
idealised placement does: at the best quote of its side when that quote holds less than
$\uniformDepth$, one tick inside it when it holds $\uniformDepth$. It is read on the idealised
book, so it is $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$-measurable.
}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{The agreement rule}
\begin{lemma}
\label{lemma.agreementRule}
 If the books coincide just before $\arrivalTimes[]_n < \breachTime$, they coincide after it
 iff $\event[n]$ is a market type, or a limit type with $\priceOffset_n = \targetOffset_n$.
\end{lemma}

Given $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$ and the past offsets:
$\offsetParameter(1-\offsetParameter)^{\targetOffset_n}\indicator{\targetOffset_n \geq 0}$
\note{
A market buy is $(\arrivalTimes[]_n, 1, \infty, +1)$ under the geometric placement, and a limit
buy of one share at the best ask under the idealised one, whose market part is its whole size
by the decomposition of class 1. The best ask holds at least one share, so both trade one share
there and rest nothing.
For a limit order of direction $d$, with $\tilde{p}_n$ the idealised price,
$d(p_n - \tilde{p}_n) = \tickSizeOfLOB(\targetOffset_n - \priceOffset_n)$, so equal offsets give
the same message. Otherwise the size resting at $p_n$ differs by one between the two books. The
probability is the geometric law at $\targetOffset_n$, the offsets being independent of the flow
and of one another.
}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{Before the first discrepancy the books are one}
\begin{prop}
\label{prop.coincidenceBeforeDiscrepancy}
 The books, and their mid-prices, coincide on $[0, \discrepancyTime)$. $\discrepancyTime$ is
 the first limit event before $\breachTime$ with $\priceOffset_n \neq \targetOffset_n$, else
 $\breachTime$.
\end{prop}

$\discrepancyTime$: the first $\arrivalTimes[]_n < \breachTime$ after which some size differs

On $[0, \discrepancyTime)$: the geometric book satisfies the assumption
\note{
By induction on the events, with the agreement rule deciding each one while the books coincide.
The mid-prices may still coincide after $\discrepancyTime$: an order resting behind the best
quote of its side changes the book and not the mid-price. So the first discrepancy is at most
the first time at which the two mid-prices differ.
}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{The conditional survival law}
\begin{prop}
\label{prop.couplingSurvival}
 \[
  \Prob\big(\discrepancyTime > t \,\big\vert\, \sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}\big)
  = \offsetParameter^{\countingProc[\limitTypes](t)}
  (1-\offsetParameter)^{\cumulativeTargetOffset(t)} \indicator{\breachTime > t}
 \]
\end{prop}

$\countingProc[\limitTypes] = \countingProc[3] + \countingProc[4]$: the count of limit orders

$\cumulativeTargetOffset(t)$: the sum of their target offsets, $\infty$ at a $-1$

A functional of the idealised path alone
\note{
The event that the books survive to $t$ is the intersection of $\{\breachTime > t\}$ with the
events $\{\priceOffset_n = \targetOffset_n\}$ over the limit events of $(0, t]$. The first event
and every target offset are $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$-measurable, the offsets are
independent of $\sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}$ and of one another, and the conditional
probability is the product of the agreement probabilities.
Read factor by factor: a limit order parts the books with probability $1 - \offsetParameter$
when it joins a best quote facing a spread of one tick, with a probability that grows with the
spread otherwise, and with certainty at a full best quote facing a spread of one tick.
}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{Every factor is at most $\offsetParameter$}
\begin{remark}
\label{remark.couplingAtUnitOffsetParameter}
 \[
  \offsetParameter^{\countingProc[\limitTypes](t)}
  \indicator{\breachTime > t, \, \cumulativeTargetOffset(t) = 0}
  \leq \Prob\big(\discrepancyTime > t \,\big\vert\, \sigmaAlgebra^{\multiCountingProc}\big)
  \leq \offsetParameter^{\countingProc[\limitTypes](t)}
 \]
\end{remark}

$\offsetParameter = 1$: $\discrepancyTime$ the first limit event with $\targetOffset_n \neq 0$
\note{
Equality holds in each factor at a target offset of zero. At $\offsetParameter = 1$ every offset
is zero almost surely, and the first discrepancy is a function of the path: the first limit
event before the breach whose target offset is not zero. The path does not depend on
$\offsetParameter$, so by bounded convergence $\Prob(\discrepancyTime > t)$ converges to its
value at $\offsetParameter = 1$ as $\offsetParameter \uparrow 1$.
The exercise of the notes: show that the number of limit orders before the first discrepancy is
at least $k$ with probability at most $\offsetParameter^{k}$, deduce that its mean is at most
$\offsetParameter/(1-\offsetParameter)$, and say on which paths the first bound is an equality.
Averaged over the paths, the upper bound is
$\Expectation[\offsetParameter^{\countingProc[\limitTypes](t)}]$, which the exponential kernel
computes in closed form.
}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{The generating functional of the counts --- 1 of 2}
\[
 \pgfExponent\derivative(r)
 = -\decay\,\pgfExponent(r)
 + \excitation^{\transpose}\big(\pgfArgument(r) \circ e^{\pgfExponent(r)} - \mathbf{1}\big),
 \qquad \pgfExponent(0) = 0
\]

$\pgfArgument$: piecewise constant, with values in $[0,1]^{\numEventTypes}$

$r$: the time left to the end of the window

$\pgfExponent$: defined on $[0,\infty)$, in $[-\branchingMatrix^{\transpose}\mathbf{1}, 0]$
\note{
A Riccati equation, with $\circ$ the entrywise product and the exponential taken entrywise. The
entry $\pgfExponent_{\eone}$ is indexed by the exciting type: an event of type $\eone$ begets
events of type $e$ at the rate $\excitation\subscriptee e^{-\decay s}$, which is why the
transpose appears.
The box is invariant: on the face $\pgfExponent_e = 0$ the field points inwards, and on the face
$\pgfExponent_e = -(\branchingMatrix^{\transpose}\mathbf{1})_e$ it is at least
$\decay(\branchingMatrix^{\transpose}\mathbf{1})_e - (\excitation^{\transpose}\mathbf{1})_e = 0$.
The field is locally Lipschitz, so the solution exists for all $r$.
}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{The generating functional of the counts --- 2 of 2}
\begin{prop}
\label{prop.countGeneratingFunctional}
 \[
 \begin{gathered}
  \Expectation\Big[ \prod_{s < \arrivalTimes[]_n \leq s+\forecastHorizon}
  \pgfArgument_{\event[n]}(s + \forecastHorizon - \arrivalTimes[]_n)
  \,\Big\vert\, \sigmaAlgebra_s \Big]
  \\
  = \exp\Big( \pgfExponent(\forecastHorizon)^{\transpose}\decayedCounts(s+)
  + \int_0^{\forecastHorizon} \baseIntensity^{\transpose}
  \big(\pgfArgument \circ e^{\pgfExponent} - \mathbf{1}\big)\,dr \Big)
 \end{gathered}
 \]
\end{prop}

Constant $\pgfArgument$: the generating function of the counts

$\branchingRatio < 1$, stationary, $\pgfArgument = \mathbf{1}$ past $\forecastHorizon$: the integral to $\infty$
\note{
The proof is a martingale. Multiply the product over the events of $(s, u]$ by the exponential
evaluated at the time left, $r = s + \forecastHorizon - u$, and at the state $\decayedCounts(u+)$.
Between events its logarithm drifts; at an event of type $e$ it is multiplied by
$\pgfArgument_e e^{\pgfExponent_e}$. Compensating the jumps with
$\intensity[ ] = \baseIntensity + \excitation\decayedCounts$, the terms in $\baseIntensity$
cancel and the drift is the Riccati equation applied to $\decayedCounts$, which vanishes. The
process lies in $[0,1]$, so it is a martingale, and its values at $u = s + \forecastHorizon$ and
$u = s$ are the two sides.
The exercise of the notes: differentiate at $\pgfArgument = \mathbf{1}$ in the direction of
$\unitVector_{\eone}$ and recover the forward mean of the counts of class 3.
}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{The decoupling rate}
\begin{corol}
\label{corol.decouplingRate}
 For $\branchingRatio < 1$,
 $\frac{1}{\forecastHorizon}\log\Expectation\big[
 \offsetParameter^{\countingProc[\limitTypes](s+\forecastHorizon)
 - \countingProc[\limitTypes](s)} \,\big\vert\, \sigmaAlgebra_s\big]
 \to -\decouplingRate(\offsetParameter)$, with
 $\decouplingRate(\offsetParameter) = (1-\offsetParameter)\stationaryIntensity[\limitTypes]
 + O\big((1-\offsetParameter)^{2}\big)$.
\end{corol}

$\pgfArgument = \mathbf{1} - (1-\offsetParameter)\mathbf{1}_{\limitTypes}$, constant

$\decouplingRate(\offsetParameter) = -\baseIntensity^{\transpose}
(\pgfArgument \circ e^{\pgfExponent_\infty} - \mathbf{1})$, with
$\pgfExponent_\infty$ a zero of the field
\note{
At a constant argument the derivative of $\pgfExponent$ solves a linear system with non-negative
off-diagonal entries from a non-positive start, so $\pgfExponent$ is non-increasing and converges
to a zero of the field. The integrand converges, and the state term stays bounded.
At $\offsetParameter = 1$ the zero is $0$, where the derivative of the field is
$\hurwitzMatrix^{\transpose}$, invertible; the implicit function theorem gives the branch, and
its derivative gives the first-order term, which is $\stationaryIntensity[\limitTypes]$ by the
stationary intensity of class 3.
So, to first order in $1-\offsetParameter$, the upper bound on the survival decays at the
stationary rate of limit orders times the probability that an offset is not zero. The bound is
attained along the paths on which every target offset is zero.
}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}

 \[

  - \Delta\nthBestBidSize[1] + \Delta\nthBestAskSize[1]
  = \sum_{t-\ofiWindow < \arrivalTimes[]_n \leq t}
  \big( \signum(\Delta_n \bestBidPrice)\,\outerSize^{b}_n
  + \signum(\Delta_n \bestAskPrice)\,\outerSize^{a}_n \big).
 \]
\end{prop}

$\outerSize$: the size at the outer value of a moving best price

Idealised book: every move one tick, $\outerSize = \uniformDepth$

the size at each touch over the window, and $\Delta_n$ the change across the $n$-th event.

\begin{frame}
