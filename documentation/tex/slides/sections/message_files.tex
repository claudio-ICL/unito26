\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{The aggregate is the image of the stream}
\[
\nthBestAskSize[i]_t
 \; = \!\!\!\!
 \sum_{\lbrace (s,q,p,-1) \, \in \, \askOrderQueue_t \; : \; p \, = \, \nthBestAskPrice[i]_t \rbrace}
 \!\!\!\! q
\]

\pause

The exchange runs this fold before publishing.

\pause

A sum does not record its summands.

\pause

The summand reached first decides who is filled.
\note{
The two feeds are not independent products. The queues $\askOrderQueue_t$ and
$\bidOrderQueue_t$ are sets of orders and the sizes are sums over them, so the second stream
is the image of the first under those sums, computed by applying the matching rules of
section 1.1 to the engine's own message stream.
The aggregate says how much rests at a price, never how that size is split between orders,
nor which of them the matching algorithm reaches first. That split is what decides whether a
given resting order is executed, and section 1.1 sets it aside.
What survives is the quantity the chapter is about: how size has accumulated across the
levels of the two sides. That cross-section is what the queue imbalance and the order flow
imbalance of section 1.4 are computed from.
}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{The book is a fold}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq.bookFold}
 \mathcal{B}_{k} = F(\mathcal{B}_{k-1}, m_k),
 \qquad \mathcal{B}_0 = \varnothing .
\end{equation}

\pause

The book is the accumulator: one state, the current one.

\pause

The sequence $(\mathcal{B}_k)_k$ belongs to whatever drives the fold.
\note{
Writing $\mathcal{B}$ for a configuration and $m_k$ for the $k$-th message of the stream, the
book is the accumulator of a fold.
The sequence of states is not held by the book; it is produced by whatever drives the fold,
at whatever resolution is wanted. Sampling every message, every second, or only at the times
a statistic is evaluated, are three drivers of one accumulator, and none of them is a
different book.
}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{The transition is one function, not a case analysis}
\[
F\big(\mathcal{B},\,(t,q,p,d)\big)
 \; = \;
 \underbrace{\text{consume } \marketOrderSize}_{\text{market part}}
 \; \text{then} \;
 \underbrace{\text{rest } q - \marketOrderSize \text{ at } p}_{\text{resting part}}
\]

\pause

No separate branch for a marketable order.

\pause

Two code paths where the mathematics has one.
\note{
The transition $F$ is the update rule of section 1.1: the arrival of an order takes the
aggregated state to the aggregated state, with no reference to the individual orders resting
behind the sizes. What makes it a single function rather than a case analysis is the
decomposition of section 1.1.
A message is a submission, carrying an order $(t,q,p,d)$, or a cancellation, withdrawing a
size $q$ from the queue at $p$ on the side $d$; a deletion is the cancellation of all of it.
An execution is not a third kind: it is the record of the market part of somebody else's
submission, read on the passive side.
}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
  \frametitle{Each pass of the loop empties one price}
{\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}
 \REQUIRE $\mathcal{B}_{k-1}$ as two maps from price to size, and the message $m_k$
 \IF{$m_k$ is a cancellation of $q$ at $p$ on the side $d$}
 \STATE subtract $q$ from the size at $p$ on the side $d$, and remove $p$ if it reaches zero
 \ELSE
 \STATE let $(t,q,p,d)$ be the submitted order and set its market part
        $\marketOrderSize$ by the decomposition
 \STATE set $r := \marketOrderSize$
 \WHILE{$r > 0$}
 \STATE let $\pi$ be the best price-eligible price on the side $-d$
 \STATE match $r \wedge \nthBestAskSize[1]$ shares at $\pi$ if $d=+1$, and
        $r \wedge \nthBestBidSize[1]$ if $d=-1$
 \STATE subtract them at $\pi$, remove $\pi$ if it reaches zero,
        and decrease $r$ by the same amount
 \ENDWHILE
 \STATE add $q - \marketOrderSize$ to the size at $p$ on the side $d$
 \ENDIF
 \RETURN $\mathcal{B}_{k}$
\end{algorithmic}
}

\pause

Finite, because the book is.
\note{
The loop is the reduction of the update rule to its elementary pieces, and it is the
elementary stream of section 1.1 written as code.
We implement it more than once, and compare the implementations on the time and the memory
they take --- which is the whole of the second notebook.
}
\end{frame}

\begin{remark}
\label{remark.gridAgainstOccupied}
 The queue imbalance $\queueImbalance[n]$ is recoverable from a truncated aggregated book
 only for those $n$ within which no gap falls.
\end{remark}
\note{
The index $i$ of $\nthBestAskSize[i]$ counts levels of the price grid, whereas an aggregated
feed reports the first $L$ \emph{occupied} prices of each side. The two agree only when the
occupied prices are contiguous on the grid.
Here the second column names a price two ticks from the touch, and the two indexings part
company from $i=2$ onwards. Summing the first $n$ size columns of a file is therefore a
different statistic, which the first notebook measures: on the reference book it reads
$0.54$ where $\queueImbalance[2]$ reads $0.14$, and both lie in $[-1,1]$.
}
